## A1. Nevada State Contractors Board (NSCB): licensing, limits, exemptions, recovery fund, contract and ad rules

### Takeaway
Any job of $1,000 or more (labor plus materials), any job needing a permit, and any plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, heating or A/C work needs an NSCB license. Each license has a Board-set monetary limit per job and client, and work cannot go over it. Residential contractors must put the Recovery Fund notice in the contract. Ads must show the contractor's name and license number. A platform should store license number, classification and monetary limit per team, put them on every quote, contract and ad, and warn before a quote goes over the limit or the $1,000 handyman line.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.031(6) exempts "Any work to repair or maintain property the value of which is less than $1,000, including labor and materials." The exemption does not apply if a building permit is required, if the work is part of a larger project of $500 or more, or if smaller contracts were split "to evade the provisions of this chapter." — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **SECONDARY.** Guides also say the exemption never covers plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, heating or A/C work. NRS 624.220(4)(b) has the same carve-out for licensees working outside their classification (below). — [contractorlicenserequirements.com](https://contractorlicenserequirements.com/nevada/handyman-license-requirements/); [koira.ai](https://www.koira.ai/licensing/handyman/nevada)
- **SECONDARY.** The NSCB issues no "handyman" license. A handyman works under the under-$1,000 exemption, and splitting one job into several invoices to stay under it counts as unlicensed contracting. — [nevadacontractors.org](https://www.nevadacontractors.org/guides/contractor-vs-handyman-in-nevada-when-you-need-a-license)
- **SECONDARY, unconfirmed.** Housecall Pro's licensing page mentions a "B-7 Restricted" license for $1,000–$7,000 jobs. No other source backs this. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/licensing/handyman/nevada/)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.220(2): the Board "shall limit the field and scope of the operations of a licensed contractor by establishing a monetary limit." That limit is the most a licensee may take on in one or more contracts on a single site or subdivision for a single client. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.220(4)(b): a licensee may work outside its classification when the value is under $1,000 (labor and materials) and no permit is needed. It still may not do plumbing, electrical, refrigeration or A/C work. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **SECONDARY / NSCB presentation.** The license classes are A (General Engineering, about 25 subclasses), B (General Building, B-1 to B-7), C (Specialty, about 42 subclasses) and AB for both. NSCB's own Business Assistance presentation gives a $200k minimum monetary limit for B and B-2. Contract values cannot exceed the license's limit, and the financial statement required grows with the limit. — [NSCB BAP presentation 2024](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Business-Assistance-Program-6.18.24.pdf); [nevadacontractorlicense.com](https://nevadacontractorlicense.com/license-types/); [contractorlicenserequirements.com GC](https://contractorlicenserequirements.com/nevada/general-contractor-license-requirements/)
- **SECONDARY.** NRS 624.247 lets the Board refuse a license for up to a year to an unlicensed person who bid on work of $1,000 or more. — [koira.ai](https://www.koira.ai/licensing/handyman/nevada)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.3017(2) makes it cause for discipline to advertise construction work without the name and license number of the responsible licensed contractor. NRS 624.3017(3) does the same for advertising work beyond the license's scope. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.600: on a single-family residence, a general building contractor must give the owner in writing (a) the name, license number, address and phone of every subcontractor and of every supplier of materials worth $500 or more, (b) notice that these parties may record a lien under NRS 108.226, and (c) the Board's informational form on contractors and liens. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **VERIFIED.** Residential Recovery Fund (NRS 624.400–.560):
  - Only a natural-person owner-occupant of a single-family home harmed by a licensed residential contractor can claim.
  - The fund pays at most $40,000 per injured person (624.510(3)).
  - Claims against one contractor are capped at $750,000 or 20% of the account, whichever is less (624.510(7)).
  - A complaint must be filed within 4 years of completion (624.480).
  - Residential contractors pay a biennial assessment: $200 for a limit up to $1M, $500 for a limit over $1M, $1,000 for unlimited (624.470).
  - — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
  - Older guides cite $35,000. The current text says $40,000.
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.520: residential contractors must put a written statement headed "RESIDENTIAL CONSTRUCTION RECOVERY FUND" in any contract for qualified services, in the prescribed form, pointing owners to the Board. Leaving it out costs up to $250 for a first violation and up to $500 after that. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **SECONDARY (I could not read the current statute text).** NRS 624.940 is the residential pool and spa contract section. Its requirements:
  - The contract must include the owner's name and address, property address, date signed, estimated completion date, a description of the work, and initial boxes.
  - Printed information must be in at least 10-point bold type.
  - Payments may never exceed 100% of the work done (apart from allowed deposits).
  - The contract must state that the NRS 624.600 notice was given.
  - The owner gets copies of everything signed and a receipt for money paid.
  - The contractor must pull permits.
  - Any waiver of these rights is void.
  - NAC 624.6958 adds more bold-type terms.
  - — [Justia NRS 624.940 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/chapter-624/statute-624-940/); [Justia 2013](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/2013/chapter-624/statute-624.940/)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.609 (prompt pay on private work):
  - The owner pays by the schedule's due date, or within 21 days of the prime contractor's payment request if there is no schedule or the agreement is oral.
  - The owner may hold back a retention of up to 5%.
  - Any withholding needs a signed written notice by the due date that gives the amount and a reasonably detailed reason.
  - The contractor may dispute it or send a notice of correction.
  - — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.610: for nonpayment, the prime contractor may stop work on at least 10 days' written notice and terminate on 15 days' notice after stopping. Remedies, including interest, are in 624.630, which I did not read. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)

### Inferences
- Pool *service* (cleaning and chemicals, no repair) and house cleaning are not "construction," so they likely need no NSCB license. Pool equipment repair or replacement of $1,000 or more likely does, and Nevada licenses pool and spa work under its own classification. **ASSUMED.** Confirm with the NSCB or NAC 624 for each trade.
- Landscaping, painting, flooring and similar work go over the $1,000 line quickly, so "handyman mode" in a platform should total labor plus materials per *project*, not per invoice. Cumulative jobs at one address should count as one project because of the split-contract rule and the $500 larger-project rule.
- The general residential contract content rules outside pools (for example a written contract, down payment caps, the 624.600 notices) could not be confirmed in primary text. A lawyer should give the full list for B, C-subclass residential remodel and repair contracts.

### Gaps
- I could not read NSCB's own pages or the NAC 624 classification list (C-1 to C-42 names and the current minimum limits per class). The pages were not retrieved.
- NRS 624.700/.720 text was not retrieved. 624.720 (unlawful advertising, disconnecting the phone number listed) is known only by its title. Whether the license number must be on *bids* and *contracts* (not just ads) is unconfirmed. Many guides say it must be; treat it as best practice.
- The current down-payment limit for residential contracts (if any outside pools) is unconfirmed.

## A2. Business licenses and taxes (state, Clark County, cities; sales/use tax; Commerce Tax; MBT)

### Takeaway
A Las Vegas trade business needs a Nevada State Business License, $200 a year ($500 for corporations), plus a county or city license for each place it works (Clark County unincorporated, City of Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas each license separately). Contractors improving real property are the *consumer* of their materials. They pay sales or use tax when they buy and must not show sales tax on the customer's invoice for a construction contract. The Commerce Tax starts above $4M of Nevada gross revenue. The MBT is a payroll tax above about $50k of wages a quarter.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED.** The State Business License renews for $500 a year for corporations and $200 for all other entities, on top of the annual list fees. — [NV SOS FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/licensing/state-business-license/state-business-license-faq)
- **SECONDARY.** The late renewal penalty is $100. — [startbusinessbystate.com](https://startbusinessbystate.com/nevada/business-license/)
- **VERIFIED (county page, partial).** Every Clark County license application has a $45 non-refundable application fee. Fees vary by category. The county's category list shows a general contractor category at $150 for work that needs no NSCB license. A state license does not replace a county or city license. — [Clark County license fees](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/business/doing_business_with_clark_county/business-license-fees); [Clark County category list PDF](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:9a25dd5c-f248-47ec-9b06-e359e56593b0/original/as/list-of-categories-and-fees.pdf); [General license guide](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/business/doing_business_with_clark_county/divisions/general_business/general-license-guide)
- **SECONDARY.** An older third-party schedule lists Contractors (NAICS 236000) at $100 with May and November due dates. It may be out of date. — [ACCLV schedule](https://www.acclv.com/uploads/7/9/0/4/79049866/ccbl_license_categories_fee_schedule.pdf.xlsx.pdf)
- **VERIFIED.** The contractor is the consumer of materials used to improve real property and owes tax on the materials only (NAC 372.200). A contract that includes fabrication and installation is not a retail sale. — [NAC 372.200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/nevada/NAC-372-200); [NV Tax Construction Contractors](https://tax.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Construction_Contractors.pdf)
- **VERIFIED (Dept. of Taxation guidance).** "If any tax is shown on the invoice the Department will consider it a retail sale rather than a construction contract and the contractor will be liable to the Department for that tax." Materials bought from out-of-state vendors owe use tax on the contractor's return. — [NV Tax Construction Contractors](https://tax.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Construction_Contractors.pdf); [NV Tax Construction page](https://tax.nv.gov/education/construction/)
- **SECONDARY.** The Clark County and Henderson combined sales tax rate is 8.375% (6.85% state plus 1.525% local). — [QuickBooks NV guide](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/nevada/business-tax-guide-nevada/)
- **VERIFIED (statute exists) / SECONDARY (figures).** Commerce Tax (NRS 363C) applies to Nevada gross revenue above $4,000,000 per fiscal year (July 1–June 30) at industry rates of 0.051%–0.331%. Sources disagree on whether businesses under $4M must still file. — [NRS 363C](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-363C.html); [openaccountants](https://www.openaccountants.com/skills/nv-commerce-tax); [howmuchbusiness.tax](https://www.howmuchbusiness.tax/states/nevada)
- **SECONDARY.** The Modified Business Tax (general businesses) is about 1.17% on quarterly gross wages above about $50,000. — [QuickBooks NV guide](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/nevada/business-tax-guide-nevada/); [cleertax](https://www.cleertax.com/resources/corporate-state-taxes/nevada/)

### Inferences
- **Invoice design matters for tax.** For real-property work, the default invoice should show no "sales tax" line. Retail sales of goods with no installation (for example a part sold over the counter, or a filter delivered) do need tax collected. A platform needs a per-line "taxable retail vs. construction contract" setting, with construction lines carrying no tax. **INFERRED** from the Dept. of Taxation guidance. Confirm with a CPA, especially for service and repair work that isn't clearly a real-property improvement (for example appliance repair, where labor may be untaxed but parts taxable).
- A team working in Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas likely needs several local licenses. A license and expiry tracker per jurisdiction is useful. **ASSUMED.** The city rules were not researched.

### Gaps
- City of Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas business license fees and contractor rules were not retrieved.
- The current MBT rate and threshold were not confirmed on tax.nv.gov.

## A3. Mechanic's liens, lien waivers, prompt pay (NRS 108, NRS 624.609)

### Takeaway
Nevada lien law is strict and form-driven. A sub or supplier needs a Notice of Right to Lien to the owner (it reaches back 31 days). Residential claimants need a 15-day Notice of Intent before recording. The lien must be recorded within 90 days of last work (40 days after a recorded Notice of Completion) and served within 30 days, and it lapses after 6 months unless suit is filed. Waivers are enforceable only on the four statutory forms in NRS 108.2457.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.245:
  - Lien claimants other than labor-only claimants, prime contractors and those who contract or sell directly to the owner must deliver a Notice of Right to Lien to the owner, in person or by certified mail, "in substantially the following form."
  - Subs and suppliers also send a copy to the prime contractor.
  - Without it, no lien for materials or equipment (other than labor) can be perfected.
  - The notice covers work from 31 days before it is given.
  - — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.226:
  - The notice of lien must be recorded within 90 days of the latest of completion, last delivery or last work.
  - It must instead be recorded within 40 days after a recorded and served Notice of Completion (NRS 108.228).
  - On *residential* projects, every claimant except laborers must serve a 15-day Notice of Intent to Lien on the owner and the reputed prime contractor before recording, by personal delivery or certified mail. This adds 15 days to the deadline. Nonresidential projects are exempt.
  - — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.227: a copy of the recorded lien is served on the owner within 30 days of recording. NRS 108.233: the lien binds the property for no more than 6 months after recording unless suit is filed or an extension is recorded (at most 1 year). — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.228: the owner may record a Notice of Completion and must mail it within 10 days to each prime and each claimant who gave a notice. If not, it is ineffective against them. — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.237(2): a prevailing claimant gets interest at the contract rate, or if none, the prime rate at Nevada's largest bank plus 4%. — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.2453 makes these contract terms void:
  - waiving or limiting lien rights except as the statute allows
  - choosing another state's law
  - requiring disputes to be resolved outside Nevada
  - waiving delay or impact claims for unforeseen events
  - — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 108.2457:
  - A waiver is enforceable only if it is "in the following form" (no "substantially" here, unlike the notice), signed by the claimant, and, for conditional forms, the payment has been received.
  - The four forms are:
    - (a) Conditional Waiver and Release Upon Progress Payment
    - (b) Unconditional Waiver and Release Upon Progress Payment
    - (c) Conditional Waiver and Release Upon Final Payment
    - (d) Unconditional Waiver and Release Upon Final Payment
  - Conditional forms take effect when the check clears and cover only the amount paid.
  - Unconditional forms take effect immediately and must carry a prescribed notice in type at least as large as the largest type on the document.
  - A waiver given for a bounced check is void.
  - A joint check counts as payment only up to the lesser of its amount, the amount intended for the claimant, or the balance owed.
  - — [NRS 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-108.html)
- **VERIFIED.** NRS 624.609(2) lets an owner require lien releases as a condition of payment. — [NRS 624](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-624.html)
- **SECONDARY.** A claimant must give a lien release within 10 days of full payment, cited as NRS 108.2437. — [lien guides via search; e.g. Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/mechanic-lien/nevada-lien-law-faqs/)

### Inferences
- A trade platform can compute and remind on every lien deadline from the job's "first work" and "last work" dates: the 31-day look-back, the 15-day residential notice, the 90/40-day recording window, 30-day service, and the 6-month suit deadline. This is high value and low risk, as reminders rather than legal advice.
- Waiver templates must reproduce the statutory text **verbatim**, including the large-type notice. Free-form "release" text is unenforceable in Nevada. The template should be locked text with fill-in fields only.
- Prime contractors who deal directly with homeowners don't send a Notice of Right to Lien, but they do owe the NRS 624.600 sub and supplier disclosure and the lien informational form.

### Gaps
- I did not read the exact statutory form text (wording and field layout). It should be copied from the official NRS 108.2457(5) text, and a lawyer should review the template.
- Nevada's public-works prompt-pay and bond (Little Miller Act, NRS 339) rules were not researched.

## A5. Customer-facing rules: texting (TCPA / 10DLC), email (CAN-SPAM), privacy (NRS 603A), e-signatures (UETA), 3-day cancel

### Takeaway
Business texting from a 10-digit number needs brand and campaign registration with The Campaign Registry. Carriers have blocked unregistered A2P traffic since Feb 2025. Registration does not replace TCPA consent: marketing texts need prior express written consent, and opt-outs must be honored by any reasonable means. Door-to-door (home solicitation) sales in Nevada carry a 3-business-day right to cancel, which must be explained out loud and in writing.

### Cited Findings
- **SECONDARY (vendor guides; consistent with each other).** All A2P SMS over 10-digit long codes in the US must be registered with The Campaign Registry (brand plus campaign). Since February 1, 2025, US carriers block 100% of unregistered A2P traffic on 10DLC. — [MessageDesk](https://www.messagedesk.com/blog/a2p-10dlc); [alive5 2026 guide](https://www.alive5.com/2026-tcpa-and-10dlc-compliance-guide-for-business-texting)
- **SECONDARY.** Registration and consent are separate. A registered campaign can still break the TCPA. Marketing texts need prior express *written* consent, and transactional texts need documented prior express consent. — [Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/blog/tcpa-compliance-sms); [beancount.io 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/04/tcpa-a2p-10dlc-sms-marketing-compliance-2026-small-business-text-program-the-campaign-registry-consent-stop-help-keyword-state-mini-tcpa-florida-ftsa-texas-sb140-guide)
- **SECONDARY.** The FCC's "one-to-one consent" rule (lead generators) was vacated by the 11th Circuit in January 2025, so it never took effect. Businesses must honor revocation of consent "by any reasonable means." — [Infobip](https://www.infobip.com/blog/tcpa-compliance-sms); [subscriberverify](https://subscriberverify.com/blog/tcpa-sms-carrier-restrictions-cold-calling-2026)
- **ASSUMED.** The FCC's revocation rule (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10)) took effect April 11, 2025: opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days, and words such as "stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel, unsubscribe" count. The part requiring revocation to apply to *all* message types was delayed, last to 2026. **Status as of Nov 2026 not confirmed.**
- **SECONDARY.** States such as Florida, Oklahoma and Texas have their own "mini-TCPA" laws with quiet hours and limits on messages per day. No Nevada mini-TCPA was identified. — [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/04/tcpa-a2p-10dlc-sms-marketing-compliance-2026-small-business-text-program-the-campaign-registry-consent-stop-help-keyword-state-mini-tcpa-florida-ftsa-texas-sb140-guide)
- **VERIFIED (statute text via search).** NRS 598 (door-to-door sales):
  - The required notice says the buyer "may cancel this transaction, without any penalty or obligation, except as provided by law, within 3 business days from the above date."
  - NRS 598.280 makes it unlawful not to tell the buyer *orally*, at signing, of the right to cancel, or to misrepresent that right.
  - Under NRS 598.2801 a violation is a deceptive trade practice.
  - — [NRS 598](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-598.html)
- **ASSUMED.** The federal FTC Cooling-Off Rule (16 CFR 429) also applies to sales of $25 or more made at the buyer's home. It requires a duplicate Notice of Cancellation form, and the seller may not start work during the 3 days except in a genuine emergency, which the buyer waives in writing. — [FTC Cooling-Off Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/cooling-period-sales-made-homes-or-certain-other-locations) (not fetched)
- **ASSUMED.** CAN-SPAM (15 USC 7701) requires accurate headers, a physical postal address, a clear opt-out honored within 10 business days, and no misleading subject lines. It covers commercial email, not transactional or relationship email. — [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) (not fetched)
- **ASSUMED.** Under NRS 603A, businesses holding Nevada residents' personal information (name plus SSN, driver's license, account or card number with code, health ID, or username and password) must use reasonable security, encrypt it in transit, and notify residents of a breach "in the most expedient time possible." Breaches of more than 500 residents are reported to the AG (2025 change; verify). Operators of websites must post a privacy notice and honor opt-outs of "sale" (NRS 603A.300–.360, SB 220 of 2019; SB 370 of 2023 added consumer health data). — [NRS 603A](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-603A.html) (not fetched)
- **ASSUMED.** Nevada adopted the UETA (NRS 719). Electronic signatures and records satisfy writing and signature requirements when both parties agree to transact electronically. Statutory notices that must be in a particular type size or delivered by certified mail (for example lien notices, NRS 108.226(6)) may still need physical delivery. — [NRS 719](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-719.html) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- Consent is the crux for texting. A platform should store, per contact, the consent type (transactional or marketing), source, timestamp and the text shown. It should stop marketing texts on STOP, quit and similar keywords at once, add quiet hours by the recipient's time zone, and fill in the 10DLC brand and campaign registration for the team.
- E-signed contracts are fine. Lien notices need certified-mail or personal-delivery tracking fields, not just an e-sign record.

### Gaps
- Primary texts of NRS 603A, NRS 719, the FTC Cooling-Off Rule and the FCC revocation order were not fetched this round. NRS 598.350–.370 (cited by a solar guide as home-solicitation sections) were not confirmed.

## B2. Payments and financing norms (2026); surcharging in Nevada

### Takeaway
Digital payments are now the majority on SMB platforms (over 51% of Jobber-processed transactions in Q1 2026). Standard tools are text-to-pay links, card on file, ACH at about 1%, Tap to Pay on phone and embedded consumer financing. Nevada has no surcharge statute. Card-network rules apply: cap at cost of acceptance, disclose it, and never surcharge debit.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (Jobber press release).** Digital payments were more than 51% of Jobber-processed transactions in Q1 2026, up 7% year on year. — [PR Newswire Q1 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/home-service-demand-accelerates-in-q1-2026-as-new-work-growth-outpaces-prior-year-across-every-major-segment-302776857.html)
- **VERIFIED (vendor page).** Jobber charges 2.9% + 30¢ for card, 2.7% + 30¢ for Tap to Pay and 1% for ACH. — [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)
- **SECONDARY.** Nevada has no surcharge statute, so card-network rules govern:
  - The surcharge may not exceed the merchant's cost of acceptance (the Visa cap is 3%).
  - It must be disclosed at entry and at checkout, as a separate line on the receipt.
  - Surcharging debit cards is barred, including prepaid debit.
  - — [Nickel](https://www.nickel.com/surcharge-laws/nevada); [FeeSlicers](https://feeslicers.com/surcharge-laws/nevada); [PaymentCloud](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/credit-card-surcharge-laws-by-state/)

  One source's claim that debit surcharging is barred "by the Durbin Amendment" is imprecise; the ban comes from card-network rules. **Lawyer or processor should confirm.**
- **SECONDARY.** Jobber's Q2 2026 report shows growth coming from pricing (larger average invoices) more than from more jobs. — [PR Newswire Q2 2026](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/home-service-delivers-steady-revenue-growth-in-q2-2026-proving-its-resilience-through-a-volatile-quarter-302856785.html)

### Inferences (ASSUMED)
- Consumer financing:
  - Wisetack is embedded in Jobber, HCP and others.
  - GreenSky (now owned by a Sixth Street-led group) and Service Finance (Truist) are common for HVAC and remodel, along with Synchrony.
  - Financing at the quote raises average ticket on bigger jobs.
- Deposits:
  - Usual practice is 10–50% up front for material-heavy jobs.
  - Nevada pool contracts limit payments to no more than the work done plus allowed deposits (NRS 624.940).
  - Other residential limits should be checked by a lawyer.
  - A platform's deposit default should be configurable and warn when a deposit looks high.

### Gaps
- Wisetack, GreenSky and Service Finance terms and merchant fees, and the current Nevada AG surcharge guidance, were not fetched.

## B3. AI in the trades (2026): real vs hype

### Takeaway
Adoption is real but shallow, and trust is low. About half of contractors engage with AI and only about 11% trust it highly. Contractors want AI built into their core platform, not bolted on. The real uses today are AI call answering and booking, drafting estimates and messages, and summaries. AI dispatch optimization and photo-to-estimate accuracy are mostly marketing.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (vendor survey, 1,017 US contractors, Sept 2026):**
  - 61% prefer AI built into their core platform.
  - 40% want clearer ROI and 38% want AI trained on trade data before using it more.
  - Engagement rose from 46% (Dec 2025) to 52% (Sept 2026).
  - Only 11% place high or complete trust in their AI tools, and 58% have little or no trust.
  - — [ServiceTitan 2026 State of AI in the Trades](https://www.servicetitan.com/guides/2026-ai-in-the-trades); [ServiceTitan press](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/servicetitan-report-finds-contractors-shifting-focus-from-ai-adoption-to-implementation-and-productivity)
- **VERIFIED (vendor press).** The April 2026 residential report found only about 25% of contractors currently use AI. 74% see AI as key to efficiency. — [ServiceTitan press 74%](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/servicetitan-report-finds-74-of-residential-contractors-see-ai-as-key)
- **VERIFIED (vendor press).** 62% of commercial firms have piloted or deployed AI. Among those using it, 59% see a positive impact but only 15% a significant one with clear ROI. — [ServiceTitan commercial](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/servicetitan-report-finds-commercial-contractors-prioritizing-profitability-and-ai-adoption); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/TTAN/service-titan-report-finds-commercial-contractors-prioritizing-k4kd8numlrul.html)
- **VERIFIED (vendor page).** Jobber sells an AI Receptionist at $29/month, included in Plus. — [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)
- **SECONDARY, unsourced.** Claims that ServiceTitan's AI voice agent books 70% of calls (90% when capacity allows) and 23% more jobs have no data behind them. **Treat as hype.** — [growwstacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/servicetitan-ai-voice-agent)

### Inferences
- "Draft, then a human accepts" fits the trust gap. That is Kloomie's existing AI pattern.
- Missed calls are the clearest ROI case, so after-hours AI answering is the strongest wedge.
- AI estimates from photos are plausible for drafting line items, not for final prices.
- Any AI that texts customers inherits the TCPA and 10DLC consent duties.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) data on AI receptionist booking rates was found.

## B4. KPIs and growth stages

### Takeaway
I found no primary benchmark source this round. The KPI list and stage model below are industry convention (ASSUMED), useful for product design but not for quoting figures.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED.** Jobber's Q2 2026 report covers 100,000+ businesses: every major segment was back to revenue growth by June (Green +7.5% YoY, Construction +9.1%), driven by larger average invoices. — [Morningstar / PR Newswire Q2 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260820ca30868/home-service-delivers-steady-revenue-growth-in-q2-2026-proving-its-resilience-through-a-volatile-quarter)

### Inferences (ASSUMED)
- **Core KPIs:**
  - booked-call rate (CSR)
  - lead response time
  - quote close rate
  - average ticket / invoice
  - revenue per tech per day
  - first-time fix rate
  - callback / recall rate
  - gross margin per job (job costing with labor burden)
  - days sales outstanding / unpaid invoices
  - membership count and retention or renewal rate
  - review count and rating
  - marketing cost per lead and per booked job
  - schedule utilization
- **Growth stages:**
  1. **Solo, 1 truck.** The owner does everything. Needs a phone app with quotes, invoices, pay links, a calendar, reminders and reviews. Very price-sensitive.
  2. **2–5 trucks.** First office hire or a spouse in the office. Needs dispatch, tech mobile app, time tracking, a shared inbox and texting, a simple pricebook, and QuickBooks sync. Per-user pricing starts to hurt here.
  3. **10–30 trucks.** Dedicated CSR, dispatcher, service manager, maybe a sales or comfort advisor. Needs call tracking, capacity planning, memberships, good-better-best and financing, tech scorecards, job costing, and permissions. This is where ServiceTitan wins deals.
  4. **30+ or multi-location.** Departments, an accounting team, marketing attribution, an API and BI, multiple business units.

## Closing lists

### (1) Compliance features every Las Vegas trade platform should have
1. **License profile per team**: NSCB license number, classification(s) and monetary limit. Put them automatically on every quote, contract, invoice, business card, web page and ad template (NRS 624.3017). Warn when a quote is over the monetary limit (NRS 624.220).
2. **Unlicensed / handyman guard**: track labor plus materials per project and address. Warn at $1,000, when a permit is needed, for licensed-only trades (plumbing, electrical, HVAC, refrigeration), and when split invoices add up past the line (NRS 624.031(6)).
3. **Residential contract template** with:
   - the Recovery Fund statement (NRS 624.520)
   - the 624.600 sub and supplier disclosure (name, license, address and phone for subs and suppliers of $500 or more)
   - the lien informational form
   - for pools: the 624.940 elements in 10-point bold with initial boxes
   - a signed copy and receipt for money paid
   - the permit responsibility
   - payments never above the value of work done
4. **Lien toolkit**:
   - the Notice of Right to Lien in substantially statutory form
   - the 15-day residential Notice of Intent
   - deadline reminders (31-day look-back, 90/40-day record, 30-day serve, 6-month suit)
   - tracking for certified mail and personal delivery
   - the four NRS 108.2457 waiver forms in exact statutory text, tied to payments (conditional forms become unconditional when the payment clears)
5. **Prompt-pay tools**: payment request date with a 21-day clock (NRS 624.609), retention of 5% or less, withholding notices, and 10-day stop-work and 15-day termination notice templates (NRS 624.610).
6. **3-day right to cancel**: for home solicitation sales, the Nevada and FTC Notice of Cancellation is attached to the contract in duplicate, with an "explained orally" checkbox and no work scheduled inside the window unless an emergency waiver is signed (NRS 598.280).
7. **Texting consent**:
   - per-contact consent records (type, source, time, wording)
   - STOP and natural-language opt-out
   - quiet hours by time zone
   - 10DLC brand and campaign registration help
   - marketing kept separate from transactional texts
8. **Email**: an unsubscribe link and postal address on marketing email (CAN-SPAM).
9. **Tax-correct invoices**: per-line retail-vs-construction setting, no sales tax line on real-property work, and a use-tax report on materials bought.
10. **Surcharge controls**: credit only, never debit, capped at cost of acceptance, disclosed up front and shown as its own receipt line.
11. **Compliance vault**: state, county and city business licenses, NSCB renewal, bond, workers' comp, GL and COIs for subs and crews, all with expiry alerts. Block a sub whose license or COI has expired.
12. **Data security**: encrypt personal data, a breach-notice playbook (NRS 603A), and a privacy notice for customer-facing pages.
