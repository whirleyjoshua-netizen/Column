Getting started

Click Start free trial on kloomie.com, enter your business’s name, your name, email and a password, and you’re in. The trial lasts 14 days and doesn’t need a card.

A two-minute setup follows. Skip any step, or all of it:

  1. Just me, or me and my team? On your own, Kloomie leaves out the team steps (you can invite people any time).
  2. What kind of business? Trades and field service switch on Jobs, Map and Finance and set up a Jobs project for the kinds of work you pick. Agencies and services get a sales pipeline. Software teams get a development board.
  3. Your AI key switches on your agents and Sidekick, on your own Claude, OpenAI or Gemini account.
  4. Your website: Kloomie reads it and fills in your business profile in Company HQ, so everything your agents write sounds like you.
  5. Your calendar, so your meetings show up in your Day.

Then Home shows a Get started list made for you, which ticks itself off as you go: your business profile, your AI key, your first job (or first campaign, or first piece of work), your calendar and email, your contacts, inviting your team, and a first job for your agents. People who join a team get a short welcome list of their own: their calendar, their AI key and their agents.

Kloomie runs in your browser at app.kloomie.com. There are also desktop apps for Mac and Windows, and you can add it to your phone’s home screen. See Download.

The desktop app opens as a slim Column docked to the edge of your screen, or as a Full size window like any other app. Switch in Settings → Notifications → Window, or from the Kloomie icon in the menu bar (or tray on Windows).

Your team and roles

Inviting people

Go to Settings → Members & invites → Invite by email and type one or more addresses. Each person gets a link that works once, for 14 days. People can also join with the team’s invite code.

Roles

  • Owners run the team: billing, deleting the team, and everything admins can do.
  • Admins manage people, settings, connections and automations.
  • Members do the work: their Day, their tasks and the campaigns they’re part of.

What members can use (Settings → What members can use): owners and admins choose which Workspace tabs and tools members have (Campaigns, Boards, Folders & Docs, Contacts, Chat, Stacks, Inventory, Finance, Meeting notes, Map, Vault, Templates) and whether they see the team’s schedule. Anything switched off disappears from members’ Workspace and Tools, Kloomie refuses it to them, and what’s in it isn’t sent to their phones. Owners and admins always have everything. With Contacts on, members can see, pick and add people; only owners and admins can change or remove them.

In Settings → What members can use, owners can choose whether members see everything shared in the team or only what’s theirs, and in Apps & Integrations → Tools they switch tools on or off for the whole team.

Home and your Day

Home is the first screen. It shows what needs you (sign-offs, stuck or overdue steps), what’s on today, your campaign steps, how your campaigns are going, and what came in. With Sidekick on, Brief me sums it up in a few sentences.

Tasks is your own schedule and to-do list, and the first tab in Workspace: opening Workspace lands on it. Events from your connected calendar show up here, and time blocks you make can go back onto your calendar. Switch the view at the top: Month (the whole month: each day shows what’s on it and the tasks due; drag a task or entry to another day to move it, and tap a day’s number to open it), Week (seven days side by side, on a wide screen), Day, List (your tasks as a list). The team’s views are on their own Team page (below). Tap a day’s name to open it, tap an empty slot to add something there, and drag a block to move it.

Saved tasks. Tasks your team makes again and again can be saved once and used anytime. Tap 📋 Saved tasks at the top of Tasks to open the shelf, then + Save a task: what it says, who it’s usually for (a person or a role), how many days after it’s made it’s due, priority, repeat and who signs it off. You can also save one from any task’s ⋯ menu (Save as a saved task…). A bundle is saved tasks in order, like “New install”: each one waits for the one before it and is due some days after that one’s done. Drag a saved task or bundle onto a day in Month or Week, onto the day you’re looking at, or onto the list. Or tap it to add it today, or pick a day and a person. Saved asks: in Your team, type an ask you’ll send again (“Sum up this week’s sales and what to chase next”) and tap ☆ Save this ask: it becomes a chip under the box that sends it in one tap, for everyone on the team, and an automation’s Ask your team step can start from it. Visit setups: when you book a visit on a job, + Save as a setup keeps its kind, time, how long, who goes and the note (“Standard install: 8:00, 3 hours, Casey and Taylor, bring the drill kit”); on any job, Start from a saved setup fills them in. A calendar block (+ Calendar block) is time you keep for something, like a standup at 7:30 for 15 minutes on weekdays, or a shop day: drop it on a day and it goes on your own calendar (and your connected calendar). Anyone on the team can use a saved block on theirs. In a chat or stack, type /saved. They’re also listed in Workspace → Tools → Templates, under Saved tasks. Everyone on the team can use them; whoever made one, or an owner or admin, can change it. In Automations, a Make a task step can be made from a saved task or bundle. Changing the saved one changes what the automation makes, and one an automation or bundle uses can’t be deleted until it’s taken out of there.

Order: in Everything, Files and your stacks the newest is at the bottom, like a chat. If you’d rather see the newest at the top, turn on Settings → Preferences → Newest at the top. It’s just for you and follows you to your other devices; Chat always reads top to bottom.

Getting around: the side toolbar has the same tabs for everyone: Home, Agents and Workspace (which opens on Tasks), and Settings at the bottom. Chat is in Workspace → Tools: it opens on the right, beside the page you’re on, with your feeds list and the feed. Links, pins and notifications to a feed open it there too. Close it with ✕ and you’re back to the page. What you see in Workspace and Tools depends on what your team’s owners and admins have turned on for members.

Settings (at the bottom of the side toolbar) is a menu of short pages: You (Profile & sign-in, Notifications, Preferences, Signature), Team (Members & invites, What members can use, Roles, Team settings), Business (and Billing for owners) and Export & delete. Type in Search settings to find one, like “password” or “time zone”. On a phone, tap a page in the list, and ‹ goes back.

  • ✦ Sidekick is at the top of every page (or press ⌘J, Ctrl+J on Windows). It opens beside what you’re doing. On a phone or in a column it’s on the menu page.
  • Workspace holds the tools you use every day: Tasks (first, and where Workspace opens), Contacts, Boards (called Projects before), Campaigns, Folders & Docs (one place for both, with a Docs | Folders switch at the top), for owners and admins Automations, and, at the far right, your pins (📌 ▾) and Tools. It opens on Tasks. Tools drops down a list of the tools (Stacks, Templates, Inventory, Finance, Email, Meeting notes, Map, Vault); pick one and it opens in a split on the right, beside the page you’re on. The ‹ in the split brings the list back. On a computer, forms you open while working (adding a task, money, a visit, a contact…) open the same way, as a panel on the right with the page still there beside it; × or Esc closes it. On a phone they rise from the bottom.
  • Workspace → Tools has the rest: Chat, Stacks, Finance, Email, Meeting notes, Map and the Vault. A tool opens beside whatever you’re looking at, on the right, so you can keep working on the left (Contacts with Email open beside it, say). The ⤢ button makes it full size and back; ‹ goes back to the list and × closes it. A tool opens this way wherever you open it from: a link, Home, Sidekick or Settings. On a phone it fills the screen. Stacks opens there too; if you open another tool from inside a stack (say /vault), the stack moves over to the page and the tool opens beside it. Tools is a list: pick one and it opens on the right. Point at a tool to see what it’s for.
  • Whiteboard (Tools → Whiteboard) is a shared planning board. Start blank or from a template (Workflow map, Campaign brainstorm, Mind map). Add stickies, text, shapes, frames that group things, pictures and files, and cards for a real task, campaign step, job, contact or doc. A card shows where that thing stands now, and tapping it opens it. Join things with arrows and write on an arrow. Drag the background (or hold Space and drag) to move around, pinch or use + and − to zoom, and Shift+drag to select an area. Keys: S sticky, T text, R shape, F frame, L arrow, Enter to write, Delete, ⌘D duplicate, ⌘Z undo, ⇧1 fit everything. Everyone who can see the board sees changes as they happen, and the faces at the top show who’s here. A board is the team’s, only yours (⋯ → Make it only mine), or a campaign’s, seen by whoever sees the campaign. Deleted boards wait 30 days in Deleted. Owners and admins can switch Whiteboard off for the team in Apps & Integrations → Tools, or for members in Settings → Team.
  • Company HQ, your Business card and Signatures are in Settings → Business, and open beside the page in the same way.
  • Your workspaces: Your workspace’s name at the top opens your workspaces: switch to another one you’re in, start a New workspace (its own people, work and free trial, on your same account) or Join a workspace with its name and invite code. Your name and email are filled in, so you only type the workspace’s name and your password.
  • News lives on Home: turn it on in Settings → Preferences → News on Home.

How the toolbar shows depends on the window:

  • Full size (a window 900 pixels wide or more): a sidebar down the left, with names. Workspace’s tabs run across the top of the page while you’re in it. The button at the sidebar’s top folds it to just icons; each device remembers.
  • Phone and column (a narrow window with a mouse, like the desktop app docked to the side of your screen): it works like a phone’s home screen.
    • Home opens first. Its cards are widgets side by side (Get started, Needs you, Today, Code, News…): swipe across to the next one, or tap a dot under them (in a column, the ‹ › arrows step through). Edit widgets at the top picks which show and in what order; each device remembers.
    • The dock at the bottom holds four apps, one tap away from anywhere: Tasks, Chat, Contacts and Boards to start with (if Boards is off for your team, the next tool takes its place). The one you’re in is lit. To change them, open the menu and tap Edit the dock, or press and hold the dock. The dock steps aside while you type.
    • ☰ at the top left opens the menu page: every page and tool as an app icon, in groups (Workspace, Tools, More with Agents, Sidekick, Apps & Integrations, Settings and Help, and Pinned). Tap one to open it; ☰ again goes back.
    • Away from Home, a house icon at the top takes you back. The gear at the top right opens Settings, next to the bell.

Pins: keep one particular job, project, campaign, chat feed, stack or doc one tap away. Open its ⋯ menu and choose 📌 Pin to Workspace. At full size your pins are in the 📌 ▾ drop-down at the right of Workspace’s top bar, next to Tools (in a narrow window or on a phone, under Pinned on the menu page, and you can put one in the dock). Each keeps its current name and is marked while it’s open. At full size (the desktop app or a browser), a pin opens in the split on the right, beside the page you’re working on, the same way the tools do: check a job while you plan your week, or keep a crew’s feed open next to a campaign. The split’s ✕ closes it, and its ⤢ makes it full size. Opening another pin swaps it in. A pin for the page you’re already on opens right there, and picking that page from the tabs moves it back over from the split. In a narrow window or on a phone, a pin opens as the page. Change your pins… at the bottom of the drop-down (or Settings → Preferences → Pinned) lets you pin a project, campaign, feed or stack, reorder them or unpin them. Right-click a pin (or press and hold it on a phone) for its menu. You can have up to 12, and only you see them.

Notifications

The bell at the top lists what’s for you: someone @mentioning you (in chat, a doc or a comment), a task or campaign given to you, “you’re up” when what you were waiting on is done, files a customer sent through one of your links, and quotes a customer accepted or declined. A number on the bell means new ones; opening it marks them seen on all your devices.

To get these on your phone or computer even when Kloomie is closed, turn on Settings → Notifications on each device (or tap Turn on in the bell). By default you also get every new message and file in the chats and stacks you’re in; switch off New messages and files too if you only want what’s for you. While you have Kloomie open in front of you, nothing buzzes your other devices; the app shows it right there. Send a test checks it works.

iPhone and iPad: Apple only lets an app from the web send notifications once it’s on your Home Screen (iOS 16.4 or later). In Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, open Kloomie from the new icon, and turn notifications on there. Desktop app: it shows notifications while it’s running, including from the tray.

Stacks

Stacks is where your team’s files, notes and links live. Drop anything in (a photo, a PDF, a spreadsheet) and it’s on every device, for everyone on the team. Make a stack for each kind of thing, like Design, Receipts or Clients, in Settings → Preferences → Stacks.

  • Find things: narrow a busy stack to photos or documents, or search it.
  • From Dropbox: click + → From Dropbox. The first time, click Connect Dropbox and allow access in the window that opens; the list fills in by itself. After that your Dropbox folders and files show right in Kloomie: open folders, search, tick the files you want and press Add. Kloomie only reads your Dropbox; it never changes anything there. Disconnect is at the bottom of the list.
  • From Google Drive or OneDrive: click + and choose where the files are. Their own picker opens; pick one or more files and they’re copied into the stack. Google Docs, Sheets and Slides come in as Word, Excel and PowerPoint files. Kloomie only sees the files you pick.
  • Share: each card can be downloaded, shared to chat, given a public link for someone outside the team, pinned so it’s never cleared, or moved to another stack.
  • Collect files: a request link lets a customer or supplier send files straight into a stack, with no account.
  • Secrets: a sealed note is encrypted on your device and opens only with a one-time code you give out.

Chat

Open Chat from Workspace → Tools → Chat. It opens beside the page you’re on. #general is for the whole team. Add a feed for each topic with the + next to Feeds, and every campaign gets its own feed. Mention someone with @ to send them a notice, react with an emoji, and reply in a thread. Press ⌘K (Ctrl+K on Windows) to search everything.

Pin things to a feed. Under the feed’s name, pin a stack, doc, folder or agent so everyone in the feed has it to hand. A pinned stack opens beside the chat: drag its cards into the chat to share them, and drag files and messages from the chat (or your computer) into it to keep them. The message stays in the chat; the stack keeps a copy. A pinned doc opens beside the chat for reading; choose Open to edit it. A pinned folder opens there too: tap a folder to go into it, drag its files into the chat, and drag things from the chat into it to keep a copy. People who can’t open something see a locked pin.

Ask an agent in the chat. Pin one of your agents to a feed (📌, then Agents). Tap its pin, or type its name after an @ (@Chief of Staff or @chief, @sales, @ops, @marketing), and ask. It says it’s on it in that message’s thread and answers there. It works as you: on your own AI key, seeing only what you can see, and anything it wants to do that needs your yes waits in Your team, which its answer links to (only you can open that link). Everyone in the feed reads its answer, and it’s told who they are, so it leaves out what they shouldn’t see. Without an AI key, it tells you where to add one.

Sign it in the chat. A PDF, Word file, photo or pasted agreement in a feed has a Sign it button. It opens ready to sign with your saved signature (you draw it the first time): tap where it goes, then Done. Anything that isn’t a PDF becomes one first. The signed copy lands in the feed, next to the original.

Ask someone in the feed to sign. Next to Sign it is Ask someone in this feed to sign. Tap where they sign, choose Next, then pick the person. Only people in that feed are listed. They get an @mention in the feed with a link that only they can use to sign, inside Kloomie. The signed copy lands in the same feed. To ask someone who isn’t on your team, choose Someone outside the team? and they get an email link.

Linking things: click 🔗 in the message box (or type [[) to link a job or any record, a project, a visit, a doc, a task, a file, a stack, a campaign, meeting notes, an agent assignment, a contact or Company HQ. It shows in the message as a card with what matters (a job’s status, date and value; an invoice’s amount and what’s due) and opens in one tap. Most of these also have Share to chat in their menu. Someone who can’t open a thing (a private project, a contact if they’re not an owner or admin, someone else’s agents) sees a plain card without its details. Your agents can post to a feed too, after you say yes, with links to what they made.

Shortcuts: type / at the start of any chat or stack box for a list of commands that go straight into a tool instead of posting. For example /task call Bob back, /doc Smith quote, /spent 45 gas, /budget Gas 400, /bill Electric 140, /contact Pat Lee, /vault gate code 4471 (it asks for your vault passphrase right there if it’s locked), or /finance and /map to open a tool.

Campaigns

A campaign is a project with a finish line: a launch, an outreach push, a seasonal promotion. It’s made of goals, and goals are made of steps.

The campaign page

A campaign opens as its own command center. At the top: its name, health (from the work, or the last check-in), dates and who’s responsible, then numbers from the real work: how much is done, its phases, its tasks, what’s blocked (waiting on something) and what’s overdue. Tap a number to see those tasks. + Add to campaign adds a phase, a task, a target, a sub-campaign, or a plan suggested from the brief. Five tabs:

  • Overview: the brief in four parts (Objective, Audience, Strategy and Success metrics, which are the targets you set, with their numbers), each edited by clicking it. Then the phases as connected cards (each shows its tasks done and progress; tap one to jump to it), each phase’s tasks below, money, and the team and roles.
  • Plan: the phases in order (drag by ⋮⋮), the targets, sub-campaigns and the team.
  • Work: the campaign’s tasks four ways, filtered to Open, Mine, Blocked, Overdue, Done or All. Tap any task to open it.
    • List: every task in one table (task, phase, who, due, priority, status).
    • Board: cards in columns by Status (Not started, In progress, Waiting, Done), Phase or Person. Drag a card to another column to change it: into progress, done, another phase or someone else. Waiting fills itself: a task leaves it when what it waits for is done.
    • Timeline: a bar for each task from the day it starts to the day it’s due, grouped by phase, with the phase’s span and progress above its tasks, today as a line, the campaign’s start and end dashed, and arrows for what each task waits for (red when it’s due before the thing it waits for). Drag a bar to move it, pull either end to change when it starts or is due, or drag across a task’s empty row to give it days. Days or Weeks zooms. A task’s Starts day is also in the task itself, above Due.
    • Calendar: a month with each task on its due day. Drag one to another day (a task with a start day keeps its length), or drag a task with no due day from the row above the month onto a day.
  • Resources: the campaign’s feed, files and notes, what each phase hands its people, and its lists and docs.
  • Activity: the log of calls and emails, Write a report, and what was recently done.
  • Report: what the work itself says, worked out from the tasks (no AI):
    • How much is done, the forecast finish at the pace of the last two weeks (and how many days before or after the end date that is), how many finished on time, what’s overdue, and what’s gone quiet.
    • Progress over time: a chart of tasks done against all the tasks, day by day, with the end date marked. Hover for a day’s numbers.
    • Phases: when each started and finished, how many days it took and its due day. For a campaign started from an SOP, Usually is how long that phase took in earlier runs, and a phase much slower than usual stands out.
    • People: what each person has done and has open, what’s overdue, how many days a task takes them and how often they finish on time.
    • Gone quiet: open tasks that aren’t waiting on anything and haven’t moved in 3 days. Nudge leaves a comment asking for news, which tells whoever it’s for; Assign opens one with nobody on it.
    • Money and logged calls by outcome, when there are any. 🖨 Print prints the report on its own.

At full size, Campaign pulse sits on the right (under the page when it’s in the split or beside one): health, the next milestone, blockers, overdue, the forecast finish, how many tasks have gone quiet, the team, and What to do next, which also suggests nudging the task that’s been quiet longest and warns when the pace runs past the end date, where each suggestion has its button (assign the tasks nobody is on, see what’s overdue, suggest tasks for an empty phase, check in), plus Ask Sidekick.

Test it first (a test run)

Run a campaign for real before it reaches the team. On its page, tap 🧪 Test run and pick the testers (you, to start). In the builder, the last stage, Resources & launch, lets you Start with a test run instead of going live. While it’s a test, a banner says so and its tasks show TEST:

  • Its tasks go only to the testers. Each person or role on a task is matched to a tester, the same one every time.
  • Only testers are told anything about it.
  • What its automations would send or change (emails, texts, Teams posts, invoices, money, stock, visits, other apps, asking an agent) and moving its job’s stage are written in the Test log instead. Emails and texts you send yourself still go out.
  • Change its phases and tasks as you go. ⏭ Skip ahead finishes a phase’s tasks so you can see what opens next.
  • 🚀 Go live (tap twice) ends the test. Its tasks go back to the people they’re for, unticked, without the test’s comments and history. What you changed stays, tasks you added stay, and tasks its automations made in the test are removed.

Sketch it on a board

In a campaign’s Plan tab, switch Phases | Board to Board. The first time, start the campaign’s whiteboard from Workflow map, Campaign brainstorm, Mind map or Blank. Whoever sees the campaign sees its board.

  • ◆ Make phases: draw a shape for each phase, with its name in it, and join them with arrows. Make phases turns every shape (or just the shapes and stickies you selected) into the campaign’s phases, in the order the arrows go. A phase an arrow points to waits for the one it comes from. The shapes become live phase cards in the same spot, and the arrows stay.
  • ☑ Make tasks in…: select stickies, pick a phase, and each becomes a task in it, shown as a live task card.
  • ⌘Z undoes the change on the board (the phases and tasks stay; delete them from Phases if you don’t want them). ⤢ Full page opens the board on its own.

Making one

Go to Campaigns → + New, or ask Sidekick to plan one with you. Sidekick suggests goals, steps, who does what, and the order things happen in. You can change anything before you build it.

On a computer, + New (and Edit details on a campaign) opens a full page beside the sidebar, with a live summary next to it. On a phone it’s one column. It’s in four stages, one at a time with Back and Next (tap any to jump): Foundation (start from, name, objective, audience, strategy, dates, the job), Structure (its steps, or sketch it on a board after it’s made), Execution (who runs it, roles, what gets logged) and Resources & launch (budget, and go live or start with a test run). Its sections: Start from (one of your saved campaigns), What it is, What it’s for (the job on a board it’s for, what it’s part of, what it comes after), Who’s on it (who runs it, the roles and who plays each, and whether it’s private), Steps (each can finish one of the job’s stages, and they can be worked in order), What gets logged and Money.

Open a step or a task

Click a step’s name to open it beside the page: Details (its name, due date, what it waits for, the job stage it finishes and what it needs), Resources, its Tasks and, for owners and admins, Automations.

Click any task to open its drawer, there or in Tasks:

  • Assignee, Due, Priority and Status: Not started, In progress or Done. A task that’s waiting on something says Waiting and can’t be started yet. Rename it at the top.
  • Description: what “done” looks like, links, anything they need to know.
  • Checklist: + Add item, tick as you go. It keeps who ticked what, and the task’s row shows ☑ 1/3.
  • Subtasks: tasks of their own, in the same step. The task can’t be finished while its subtasks are open. Deleting a task keeps its subtasks.
  • Dependencies: what it waits for (change it here) and what finishing it opens.
  • From the step: the step’s call script, notes, docs and links.
  • Linked: its step, job, the doc it came from, map pin and files.
  • Comments: the people the task is for (and whoever made it) are told; only the writer or an admin can delete one.
  • Activity: who gave it, re-dated it, sent it for sign-off and finished it.
  • Duplicate (with its checklist unticked), Delete, ✦ Tools and Put on schedule.

A saved campaign keeps its tasks’ descriptions, checklists (unticked) and subtasks.

Work orders

Any task can be a work order for the person doing the job: open it and tap 📋 Make it a work order. It gets a number (WO-1001, then WO-1002…), shown on its card and row.

  • Instructions and steps: the task’s description becomes its Instructions and its checklist its Steps.
  • Materials: add what the job takes, picked from Inventory (or typed) with how many. Ticking an Inventory item as used takes it out of stock, from the place that has enough, logged against the job and the task. Typed ones are just ticked.
  • The customer’s sign-off: with Needs the customer’s signature on (it starts on for a task on a job), hand over the phone or tablet: they sign on the pad and you write their name. It keeps who signed, when and who handed it over.
  • Can’t be finished early: a work order can’t be marked done until every step is ticked and, when it needs one, the customer has signed. That holds everywhere, the public API and automations included.
  • 🖨 Print makes a paper work order: your company, the number, the job, customer and address, who it’s for, the instructions, the steps with what’s ticked, the materials and the signature.
  • A saved campaign (SOP) keeps each work order’s setup (its materials and whether it needs a signature), so every run gets its own numbered work orders, nothing used or signed yet.

SOPs: your standard way of doing it

A campaign you save (its ⋯ → Save as an SOP) becomes one of your team’s SOPs, listed under SOPs · saved campaigns at the bottom of Campaigns. Start one by hand (Start now), for a job (the job’s + Campaign), on a weekly schedule, or from an automation. Each run is a new campaign.

  • 📖 Read opens it as a guide for training a new hire: the brief, the roles, then each phase in order with its tasks, how to do each one, its checklist, what needs a sign-off and the phase’s scripts and links. 🖨 Print prints just the guide.
  • I’ve read it: each person taps I’ve read version 3 (say) at the bottom. The list shows “You haven’t read it yet”, or “Updated since you read it”, and a count of SOPs to read. Whoever saved it, and owners and admins, see Who’s read it and Ask whoever hasn’t, which sends each of them a notice that opens the guide.
  • Make it better as you go: when a run goes better than the SOP (you added a step, fixed a script, changed the timing), open that campaign’s ⋯ → Update the SOP from this campaign and say what changed. The SOP becomes the next version, set up the way that campaign is; its name, schedule and who’s responsible stay. From the SOP’s ⋯, Update from a campaign does the same from any campaign. Everyone’s “read” now shows the older version, so ask them to read it again.
  • Versions: the guide lists every version with what changed, who and when. Go back to this (tap twice) makes an earlier one current again, as a new version. The last ten can be gone back to.
  • Your agents follow them: Sidekick and your agents can read your SOPs, and use them when planning or doing that kind of work.

What a step hands its people

On a step’s Resources tab, whoever runs the campaign (or an admin) adds what the people doing it need: + Note or script (write a call script or instructions right there), + Doc or file (one you have, or a new doc), + Template (a saved email, text or reply) or + Link. The step and every one of its tasks show it, like 📝 Call script, and tapping it opens it to read. A member opening their task lands on From the step. A saved campaign keeps its steps’ resources each time it runs.

Automations for a step

Owners and admins see an Automations tab on an opened step (or the step’s ⋯ → Automations for this step…). Turn on a ready-made one in a tap: tell whoever it’s for when their task opens, remind them if a task isn’t done 2 days after it opens, post in the campaign’s feed when the step is finished, or tell the owners when a task here is done. + New automation for this step opens the builder with the step already filled in. A step’s call script and other resources are on its tasks by themselves, so no automation is needed for that.

Steps that wait on other steps

A step can wait for another to finish (“print flyers” waits on “approve the design”). Waiting steps show what they’re waiting on. When the last thing a step waits on finishes, the person doing it is told “You’re up”. A step can also be due a number of days after it opens.

Roles in a campaign

Give a campaign roles (Designer, Caller, Approver) and put people in them. Steps can go to a role, so when people change, the work follows. A step can need a sign-off: it goes to the approver, who approves it or sends it back with a note.

Steps that use a tool

A step can be an email to send, a document to sign, a payment, files to collect, calls to make, or a meeting. Opening the step opens that tool, and finishing in the tool finishes the step.

Keeping track

Each campaign shows whether it’s on track, at risk or off track. Whoever runs a campaign can post a check-in. Overview shows every campaign at once, and what needs you. Turn on Keep me posted to follow one.

Saving and repeating

Save a campaign to start it again later, on a schedule, or straight after another one finishes, so a series runs on its own.

A campaign for a job

A campaign can be the work for one job: open the job and tap + Campaign to start a saved campaign for it (your “Blinds install”, say), or a new one. Its steps and tasks are the job’s, so they show on the job, and the campaign shows 📋 For the job. An automation can do the same: when a job moves to a stage, Start a saved campaign starts it for that job, and any task it makes is that job’s task.

Steps that move the job on: in a job’s campaign, open a goal’s ⋯ and choose Finishes the job’s stage (Measure → Measured, say). Finishing that step’s tasks moves the job to that stage. Until then the job can’t be moved there, or past it, by anyone, the API or an automation: Kloomie says which steps are still open. An owner or admin can Move on anyway with a reason, which goes on the job’s timeline. Moving a job back is always fine. A job’s campaign finishes itself when all its steps are done, and is back on if a task is reopened. Saved campaigns keep each step’s stage.

What a step needs: open a goal’s ⋯ and choose What it needs to list what the step takes besides its tasks: materials (an item from Inventory and how many), equipment (checked out to the job), a visit of a kind, done, or a field on the job filled in (the customer’s sign-off, photos). They show as chips under the step and tick themselves off as it happens: stock counts when it’s used for that step (from Use stock for it on the chip or on one of the step’s tasks). In a job’s campaign the step isn’t finished, and the job doesn’t move on, until they’re all got. Tap a chip to use the stock, book the visit or open the job; the campaign’s owner or an admin can tick one off by hand when it came another way. Once got, materials, equipment and visits stay got, even when the gear comes back. Saved campaigns keep what each step needs.

Materials on order: when a step’s materials aren’t there yet, tap the chip and choose Order it for this step (owners and admins). The order is for that job and step, and the chip says how much is on order until it comes in and is used.

Which jobs are held up: Campaigns → Overview lists every job with a campaign going: its stage, how many of its steps are finished, the next step, and what’s holding it up. That can be a step whose tasks are done but whose materials, equipment, visit or field aren’t, a task that’s overdue, a sign-off waiting, or an automation that was held back from a stage. Tap a line to go to it, or All jobs to see the ones moving along too. Owners and admins also see automations whose last run failed and connections that need connecting again. Everyone sees only the jobs and campaigns they can see.

Automations for a job’s work: an automation set off by a task or a campaign knows its job: {record} is the job’s name and {step} the step’s, and In a project narrows it to that project’s jobs (only for automations whose maker can see the job). It can also go off when A step in a campaign is finished, A task is reopened, A campaign is started or A job is held back from a stage. Whatever an automation changes on a job shows on the job’s timeline as ⚙ the automation, and so does an automation held back.

Contacts

Contacts is the team’s shared list of people: customers, leads and partners. Add people by hand, or Import a spreadsheet saved as CSV (from Excel, Google Sheets or another app). On a wide screen Contacts is a table: tap a column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse.

Groups

Not everyone you keep is a customer. Sort people into groups, like Vendors & suppliers, Subcontractors, Inspectors, Partners, Mentors & advisors, Legal & accounting or Personal. One person can be in several. Groups belong to the team. Owners and admins make and change them; members who have Contacts can see them.

  • Make one: + New group in the Groups list beside Contacts (on a phone or in a narrow window, the chips across the top). Type a name and an emoji, or tap one of the ready names.
  • See one: tap a group to see only its people, with how many are in each. In no group shows everyone not sorted yet. Search also finds people by their group’s name.
  • Put people in: tick people and choose Add to group… (or Remove from the group you’re looking at). In someone’s details, tap groups in the Groups row to add or remove them, or + New group to make one right there. Someone added while you’re looking at a group starts in it.
  • Rename or delete: a group’s ⋯ menu. Deleting a group never deletes the people in it.
  • Sales stays separate: a vendor or lawyer with no stage never shows up as a lead or in sales numbers.
  • Everywhere else: a spreadsheet’s Group (or Category) column fills groups when you import, making any that are new. Google Contacts labels come in as groups. Pick a customer on a job can be narrowed to a group. A campaign’s list from Contacts can be everyone in a group. Sidekick can find people by group (“who’s our inspector?”) and add someone to one.

To bring in Google Contacts, an admin goes to Apps & Integrations → Google Contacts. Kloomie reads the contacts about every 30 minutes and never changes anything in Google. Names, emails, phones and addresses stay Google’s; the stage, tags and notes your team adds stay yours.

Leads from websites, funnels and forms: an owner or admin turns on Apps & Integrations → Leads from websites, funnels & forms, then picks their website or tool in Connect your website or tool (WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow, Shopify, ClickFunnels, GoHighLevel, Typeform, Jotform, Google Forms, Facebook lead ads and more) and follows the steps, which have your own code or address already filled in. There are four ways in:

  • Website snippet: one line pasted into your site. Your forms keep working exactly as they are, and Kloomie gets a copy of each one sent. Login forms are never copied, card numbers and similar fields are skipped, and adding data-kloomie-ignore to a form leaves it out.
  • Lead webhook: an address funnel builders and form tools send to. Any field names and shapes work (flat or nested JSON, a form, Typeform, Tally or Jotform posts).
  • Kloomie form: a page with your name and logo to link to, or embed on your site.
  • Zapier: for tools that can’t send anywhere themselves, like Google Forms or Facebook lead ads.

Each lead lands in Contacts at the stage and with the tags you choose. Anything that isn’t a name, email, phone, company or address (like “Window count”) is kept in the note as “Window count: 6”. Someone already in Contacts with the same email or phone is updated, not added twice. Owners and admins get a notification, and your automations run. Recent leads lists the last 30 that came in, from anywhere, with every field and where it went, and Send a test lead tries it without saving anything. Match fields lists the field names your forms have sent; choose where any one should go (or leave it out). New link replaces the snippet, link and address if they get abused.

Email sent to contacts from a campaign includes an unsubscribe link, and Kloomie won’t email anyone who used it.

Boards: start blank or from a template

Boards (in Workspace; called Projects before) are where your jobs live, in columns. + New board opens a blank board straight away: jobs in columns New, Scheduled, In progress and Done. Make it yours where it is:

  • Columns: click a column’s name to rename it. + Add column at the end adds one. Drag a column by its top to move it, or use its ⋯ menu to change its color, move it left or right, add one after it, or delete it. If a column you delete has jobs in it, you pick which column they move to.
  • Jobs: + New in a column adds a job there. Open it to pick its customer from Contacts, or + New contact (they’re saved in Contacts too). From a job you can schedule a visit, add a + Task, start a + Campaign for it, log calls and notes, and link it to docs and other records.
  • + Add a detail on a job adds a detail to every job on the board in one tap: scheduled date, crew, price, photos, a checklist (with steps to start), measurements, materials, price lines, a permit, a sign-off or an address. Something else… adds any other kind of field.
  • Use a template instead: on a blank board with no jobs yet, this swaps it for a template. The empty blank board is removed once the template’s board is made, and Esc takes you back to it.

Use a template (next to + New board) starts from the kinds of work below, with the lists, details and stages that work needs. Templates are just as editable. At full size it’s a page: every way to start is one row in a list (type to filter it, or use ↑ ↓), and the one you pick shows beside it with what you get, a name and Create board. Esc goes back. In a narrow window or on a phone it opens from the bottom.

  • Construction & Trades: pick the kinds of work you do (HVAC, installers, remodeling, lawn care, towing, deliveries and more). Tick We bid on jobs too to add Bids, Quotes customers accept online, Clients and a Price list to the same project.
  • Technology & Software: a development board, roadmap, sprints, releases and tests (below).
  • Creative & Agencies: pick Agency client work (client projects from Brief to Delivered, with a timeline; deliverables with revision rounds, client feedback and the client’s sign-off; proposals the client accepts online) or UGC (creators with their handles, platforms and rates; brand campaigns with usage rights; each piece of content from Brief sent to Posted and Paid, with the brand’s sign-off; and what each creator is owed). Clients and brands come from your Contacts.
  • Business Operations: Team projects (projects, tasks and the processes your team repeats, with their steps), a Sales pipeline (contacts, companies and deals) or a blank list.

Something else? Describe how you work: if none of these fit, say what you do and how a job (or order, or case) moves along, for example a tow company that takes calls from motor clubs and police, tows to its yard and charges by the mile plus storage. Sidekick drafts the lists, details and stages, using your team’s AI key (Apps & Integrations → AI). You see the draft first and nothing is made until you click Create project.

Saved your own board as a template? It shows under Your templates in the same place.

Done with a board? Its ⋯ menu has Archive board (for whoever made it, and owners and admins). It leaves the Boards list and goes to Archived at the bottom, with who archived it and when. Nothing in it is lost: its records, links and QR labels keep working. Opened, an archived board shows a banner with Restore, and Restore is also beside it under Archived.

Sign-offs and acknowledgments

Any job or record with a sign-off (trade jobs, deliverables, UGC content, or a Signature field you add to any list) can have a form for the signer to read and accept first.

  • Make a form in Kloomie: on the sign-off, click 📄 Choose a form → Make a form, or start from a ready-made one (completion sign-off, work authorization, vehicle release, deliverable approval, content usage rights). Blanks in {curly brackets} fill in from each record: {customer}, {job}, {address}, {date}, {total}, {company}, {line_items} (the price lines) or any field by its name. Lines that start with [ ] are boxes the signer can tick.
  • Upload your own form: Upload a PDF form. The signer reads its pages, then signs on a page added after them with the job’s details.
  • For every job, or just one: a form can be the list’s (every job in it uses it) or only this record’s. Need more than one form, like an authorization before the work and a sign-off after? Add another Signature field to the list and give it its own form.
  • Sign here: hand over the device. They see the whole form filled in, tick what applies, agree to sign electronically and sign with a finger.
  • Send to sign: they get an email with a link to read and sign on any device. It comes from your Gmail or Outlook if you’ve connected one, otherwise from Kloomie in your team’s name. The record shows Sent, Opened, Declined or Link expired, and you can remind, copy the link or cancel.
  • Anyone can sign: it fills in the customer, but you can change who signs (a property manager, a police officer, a brand contact).
  • When it’s signed: the sign-off fills in with the signed PDF (with a signing certificate and history), the timeline says so, the signer is emailed a copy, and the job can move to a stage you pick (Choose a form → When signed).

Business cards

Everyone on the team can have their own card: a page with their photo, title and company (with the team’s logo and colour), buttons to call, text and email them, and their own links. Open Settings → Business → Business card.

  • Make it: it starts filled in from your account. Add a title, phone, a line about you and links (website, booking page, reviews, Instagram, a quote form, a payment link), then Save and turn on. You need to have confirmed your email.
  • Share it: Share or text it opens your phone’s share menu (Messages, AirDrop, WhatsApp, email). Show QR code puts a code on your screen that any phone camera can open. Copy link for your email signature or anywhere else.
  • Tap to share: put your card’s link on an NFC card or sticker (most sellers program it for you). Anyone who taps it with their phone gets your card. On Android in Chrome, Write to an NFC tag writes a blank one for you.
  • Save contact: the person you share with saves you to their phone’s contacts in one tap, with your photo.
  • Share your info: they can leave their name, phone and email. They go into the team’s Contacts as a lead from your card (someone already there is updated, not doubled), and you’re told. Turn it off under Options. It’s only there when your team uses Contacts.
  • See what happens: how many people opened your card, saved you and shared their info, and the taps on each link.
  • Your address: change the end of your card’s link under Options. Turn the card off any time; owners and admins can switch Business cards off for the team in Apps & Integrations → Tools. If you leave the team, your card goes too.

Formulas, rollups and calendars

Add these to any list in Boards with the + at the end of a table’s columns.

  • Formula: worked out from the record’s other fields, like {Price} * {Qty} or {Value} * 1.08. Tap a field’s name to put it in. You can use + − × ÷, brackets, if(test, yes, no), round(x, places), min, max, abs and today(). Dates count in days: {Expires} - today() is the days left, and {Start} + 30 is the date 30 days on. Say whether it comes out as a number, money, days or a date. Renaming a field doesn’t break a formula that uses it.
  • Rollup: adds up the records a link field points at: how many there are, the total, average, lowest or highest of one of their fields, or how many are ticked. For example, the total cost of the parts linked to a job. It changes when one of them does.
  • Formulas and rollups are worked out by Kloomie, so they’re the same in the app, in exports, through the API and in webhooks. You can’t type into them.
  • Calendar view: any list with a date gets one from + next to its views, or Show as a calendar in a view’s menu. Pick which date it goes by (Scheduled, Due, Renews…).

Equipment and assets

Keep track of gear that moves between sites, like camera towers, generators, trailers, tools and laptops: where each one is, the job it’s at, who has it, and its lock combo. In Boards, make a new project and pick Business Operations → Equipment & assets.

  • Each item: a number (A-101, A-102…), a status (At the yard, Deployed, In repair, Lost, Retired), the kind, where it is, the job it’s at, who has it, a serial, photos and details. Add or rename fields like in any project.
  • Labels: Print labels makes a sheet of stickers, one per item, each with a QR code. Stick one on each item. When someone on your team scans it with their phone’s camera, the item opens in Kloomie, for people who can see the project and are signed in on that phone. Anyone else gets nothing. If a label is lost or copied, make a new one from the item’s ⋯ menu, and the old one stops working.
  • At the site: scan the label, then tap Move here. Kloomie saves where you are, using your phone’s location and the street address there. Or type an address. Assign it to a job (from your other projects, not private ones), tap its status, or Report a problem, which notes it and can mark it In repair.
  • Where it’s been: every move is kept, with who moved it and when. A job shows the equipment at it.
  • Lock combos and codes: a locked field keeps them on Kloomie’s server, encrypted. They show only when someone taps Show, and each look goes on the item’s timeline. The project’s maker and admins choose whether everyone who can open the project can see them, or only the maker and admins. Locked fields aren’t in exports, spreadsheets or connected apps, so keep your own copy of anything you can’t lose.
  • At a glance: how many items are at the yard, deployed and in repair. Also the items nobody has scanned or moved in 30 days, and a map of every item with a spot.
  • What it isn’t: live GPS tracking. Kloomie knows where an item was last scanned or moved, so it’s only as current as your team’s scans.

Jobs

For businesses that work at a customer’s place: installers, HVAC, plumbing, lawn care, cleaning and more. Turn on Boards in Apps & Integrations → Tools, then open a contact and click Make a job. The first time, pick the kinds of work you do. Each kind gets its own job details, checklist and stages (an installer’s run from Lead to Measured, Ordered, Installed and Paid; a service call’s from New to Done).

A job’s window is in tabs, so you see one part at a time:

  • Overview (where it opens): where the job stands (its stage, how many steps are finished, the next step and what’s holding it up), who it’s for, its details with + Add a detail, links from other boards, and quick buttons to log a call, add a task or campaign, or ask Sidekick for the next step.
  • Client: the customer’s card (call, text, email, directions) and their other jobs.
  • Work: visits, the job’s campaigns and tasks, checklists, measurements, readings, the signature and permit, and the equipment on the job.
  • Finance: Materials (the materials list and stock used), Quote (the price lines), and for owners and admins Invoices and Cost (what the job cost and its margin).
  • Files & notes: the job’s notes doc and the docs that mention it.
  • Activity: the timeline of calls, emails, notes, changes and what automations did.

A number on a tab means something there needs you (overdue tasks on Work, overdue invoices on Finance). Each job opens again on the tab you left it on. Other boards get only the tabs they have something for. Below are the parts of a job in more detail.

  • The customer: a job starts at the customer’s address, with buttons to call, text, email them and get directions. Whoever’s assigned sees these even if they can’t open Contacts.
  • On site: tick off the checklist, enter measurements (it totals openings or square feet), equipment with model and serial, materials and their cost, and readings.
  • Sign-off: the customer reads a form filled in from the job and signs, here or from a link (see Sign-offs and acknowledgments).
  • Photos and permits: before and after photos, and the permit number, status and inspection day for trades that need one.
  • Gate and lockbox codes: kept locked, not in the notes. They show when someone taps Show, and each look goes on the job’s timeline.
  • Calendar: each trade’s jobs on a month calendar by their scheduled day. Drag a job to another day to move it, or tap + on a day to add one there.
  • Where it matters: installers and builders are asked about HOA approval; builders get the year the home was built (homes before 1978 come under lead-safe work rules); lawn care and irrigation get the address’s watering group.

Service plans, customer equipment, licenses

A Jobs project also gets lists beside the jobs:

  • Service plans (repair, recurring and cleaning work): each customer’s membership or agreement, with how often, visits a year, price, billing, member discount, when it renews and when the next visit is due.
  • Customer equipment (repair work, pools, irrigation, security, EV chargers, garage doors): each customer’s own units, with make, model and serial, where it is, when it was installed, its age worked out, warranty and last service. This is the customer’s gear, not your company’s (that’s Equipment and assets).
  • Licenses & insurance live in the workspace profile, not on a board: tap your workspace’s name at the start of Workspace and choose 🪪 Workspace profile. Add your contractor license, insurance, bonds and certifications with the number, expiry and a copy of each, and use 🔔 Warn the owners to hear 30 days before one expires. 📤 Send proof makes a link a customer or GC opens to see what you picked, with a copy of each; it works for 7, 30 or 90 days (or until you turn it off), always shows things as they are now, and the team sees how often it was opened. To prove it anywhere else: switch on Show on quotes & invoices (owners and admins), and your quote and invoice pages link “Our licenses & insurance”, showing what’s current and never what’s expired; in an automation’s email, text or Teams post, tap 🪪 Attach proof of insurance (or write {proof}) and each customer gets their own link; type /proof and who it’s for in chat; or ask your AI team to send proof, and it makes the link after your yes.

Any project can add these from ⋯ → Add a list.

Visits and the team schedule

On a job, click Schedule a visit: say what it’s for (measure, install, service…), when, and who’s going. It goes on the Day of everyone going, and on their Google, Outlook or Apple calendar if it’s connected. Tapping it there opens the job, with that visit at the top and Visit done. Moving one person’s copy moves everyone’s.

The job keeps up by itself: its Scheduled day and Assigned to follow the next visit, and its stage moves forward, so booking a measure makes it Measure booked, and finishing the install makes it Installed.

The Team page is its own page: tap your workspace’s name at the start of Workspace and choose 👥 Team, right under Workspace profile (in a narrow column or on a phone, it’s in the menu under your workspace’s name at the top). It’s for owners and admins, and for members when an admin turns on Settings → Team → What members can use → See the team’s schedule. At full size it opens beside the sidebar; on a phone it’s a pop-up. It keeps up with the work while it’s open. Its tabs are Shifts and Hours (next), This week, Visits and Weeks ahead. This week shows the team’s week in numbers: for each person, the hours booked, open tasks, overdue, due this week, with no date, done this week, job visits and the campaigns they own, with a bar for each day. It counts every task they have, private ones too, but never shows what a task says. Tap someone to see their week day by day (‹ Team goes back), and use the arrows to move a week. Visits (with Boards on) has a row for each person with their visits across the day, and the jobs still waiting for one: drag a waiting job onto someone’s row at the time they’ll go to book it. Weeks ahead is described next.

A campaign’s task that nobody is given (no person, team role or campaign role) counts as nobody on it, not as the work of whoever made it. A to-do you made for yourself is still yours.

Shifts (the first tab on the Team page) is where owners and admins build the team’s work hours for a week. It opens on Day, a timeline like the Visits board: a row for each person across the hours. Drag across an empty stretch of someone’s row to draw a shift, drag a shift to move it to another time or another person, and pull either end to make it longer or shorter (it snaps to 15 minutes). Their usual hours show as a faint green band, time off is shaded, and the Who’s on strip on top counts how many people are working each half hour, with any gap in red and listed underneath. Drag a saved shift onto someone’s row to add it. Tap a shift to change its break, name or note. Week shows all seven days: tap a day to add a shift, drag a shift to another day or person, or tap a day’s name to open it on the timeline. A shift has from, to, an unpaid break, a name (Morning, Shop, On call) and a note for them. Saved shifts fill the hours in one tap, and Copy last week starts the week from the one before. Nobody sees a shift until you press Send. Then each person whose shifts changed gets one notice, on the bell and as a push, listing their shifts for the week and the hours in all. After that, changing or removing a sent shift is a draft again: it shows dashed, or crossed out, and the person keeps what they were sent until the next Send. Each row shows the person’s hours for the week, and over 40 is marked. A ! on a shift means it’s on their time off, outside their usual hours, or overlapping another of theirs. It’s a warning only, so you can still keep it. Tap a name to see or change their usual hours and time off. Members see their own sent shifts, and everyone’s if an admin lets them see the team’s schedule. Usual hours and time off stay between the person and the owners and admins.

Hours (on the Team page) works out what the team has to give in a week. For each person, their hours come from the shifts sent to them that week. Without any, it uses their usual hours, and if they haven’t set those it assumes 8 hours each weekday. Time off counts as no hours. Hours already booked (job visits and timed appointments) are taken off to show what’s free, and anyone booked past their hours is marked, with the days listed underneath: booked on time off, or more than their shift. Tasks have no length, so they’re counted, not added in. Tap someone to see their days. Members see Hours only when an admin lets them see the team’s schedule, and never why someone is off.

🕒 My shifts is under your workspace’s name at the start of Workspace, for everyone. It lists the shifts sent to you for the next four weeks, week by week with the hours, and lets you set your usual hours and add time off, so whoever builds the schedule sees them.

📊 Analytics (Tools → Analytics) adds up your business’s numbers from what’s already in Kloomie. Pick the dates (the last 30 days, this month, this year or your own), and each number is shown against the same length of time just before, with a little chart of how it went week by week (month by month over longer dates). Business: money in, spending and profit (the business’s money only), invoiced, collected, owed and overdue now, quotes sent and won, and the win rate. Team & operations: tasks done, how many were on time, overdue now, visits done, days to finish and reopened, with a line for each person. Customers: new contacts and leads, and where they came from. Marketing: calls and emails logged in your campaigns, how many were interested or booked, and the sales from them. Tap any number to see the records behind it, and tap one to open it. Analytics is for owners and admins; to let members see Team & operations, turn on See the team’s numbers in Settings → Team → What members can use. Members never see money, customers or marketing, and behind a number they see only their own work. Ask about your numbers: type a question at the top of Analytics (“Why did profit change?”, “Who’s falling behind?”) and your AI team answers in Sidekick, reading the same numbers you can see; you can ask them anywhere else in Sidekick too. 🎯 Goals & alerts (owners and admins): set a goal for a number this week, month or quarter (money in $20,000 this month), and it shows on the number with how far along you are; or set an alert for a number going above or below a line (overdue tasks above 5). When a goal is reached or an alert goes off, owners and admins get a notification, once each week, month or quarter, and an automation can start on it (Workspace → Automations, under Analytics) to tell the team, make a task or anything else an automation does.

Weeks ahead (a tab on the Team page) looks four weeks forward from the real work. For each person and week it shows the hours booked (visits and timed appointments) and the tasks due. A task with a due date that’s given to someone is confirmed. One with no due date is estimated from its step’s date, or else its campaign’s end. A day booked past someone’s hours, or full with tasks still due on it, shows as overloaded. Their hours are the same ones the Hours tab uses: their sent shifts, otherwise their usual hours, otherwise an assumed 8-hour weekday, and none on time off. Tap a week to see the tasks and visits behind it, each with its job, campaign and step, and tap one to open it. Below that are the work nobody is on, bookings that overlap, what the coming steps need (materials against what’s on hand and on order, equipment against what’s free, visits nobody has booked), and the jobs likely to fall behind, with why. Work in a campaign or job you can’t see is counted but not shown.

Routes

Mileage

Map → Mileage keeps a log of business driving, for paying people back or claiming it at tax time. There are four ways to add a trip:

  • Track my mileage when you set off. Kloomie follows the drive on the map and counts the miles; press Stop and log miles when you arrive, say what it was for, and it’s saved with where you started and ended. Keep Kloomie open with the screen on while you drive: phones stop sharing location with web pages when they lock.
  • From a day’s job route: in Map → Job routes, Log these miles as mileage adds the day’s road miles (from the shop, to each job, and back) in one go. Doing it again for the same day updates that trip.
  • Add a trip → Work out the miles: type where you started and where you went (tick Round trip for there and back) and Kloomie works out the distance by road.
  • Type it in, or type /miles 23 supplies run in any chat or stack box.

The top shows the miles for this month (or last month, this year, all time) and what they come to at the team’s rate. Owners and admins set the rate (Change, next to the total), see everyone’s trips with each person’s miles, and can log a trip for someone. Download CSV gives a spreadsheet with every trip, how it was logged, and the total, ready for your accountant. Everyone else sees only their own.

Mileage in Finance: when the team uses Finance, each trip is also a business expense there (category Mileage, on the trip’s day, under whoever drove), so it counts in your totals and budgets. Changing or deleting the trip changes or removes the expense. Each trip keeps the rate it was logged at, so a new rate doesn’t change past months. Owners and admins can switch this off next to the rate.

Open Map → Job routes to see someone’s job visits for a day on the map, numbered in order, with the drive before each and the total driving. Start route in Google Maps hands every stop to Google Maps for turn-by-turn. Owners and admins set where routes start and end (the shop or office), and can look at anyone’s route; there’s a 🗺 button on each person in the Team schedule too.

When booking a visit, tick Any time that day if the time is a guess. Kloomie keeps the times you promised customers and fits the any-time visits around them in the order that drives least, and shows how much time that saves. If two promised visits are too close to drive between, it warns you. Your Day shows a line with the day’s driving, and opens the route.

Invoices and getting paid

Put what the job costs the customer in its Price lines. Then, under Invoices on the job, click Make an invoice: it starts from those lines, is billed to the job’s customer, gets the next number (INV-1001, INV-1002…) and is due in two weeks. Change anything you need, then click Send to customer. That copies a link for you to text or email them, and moves the job to Invoiced. You can see when they open it.

To let customers pay by card, connect your own Stripe account in Apps & Integrations → Card payments: paste your Stripe secret key (or a restricted key). The money goes straight into your Stripe account. Kloomie never holds it. For cash or a check, open the invoice and click Record a payment. Every payment goes into Finance as money in for the business, and paying in full moves the job to Paid. Only owners and admins see invoices.

QuickBooks: an owner or admin can connect QuickBooks Online in Apps & Integrations → QuickBooks. After that, each invoice you send goes into QuickBooks under the same number, billed to the same customer (Kloomie adds them if they’re new). The work goes in on a “Kloomie services” item, and sales tax goes in as its own line, so the totals match to the cent. Payments you take in Kloomie are recorded against it there. A payment you record in QuickBooks against one of those invoices comes back into Kloomie, and the job moves to Paid. Changing an invoice before it’s paid updates it in QuickBooks, and voiding it voids it there.

Quotes go too. A quote you send from a job goes into QuickBooks as an estimate: under the quote’s number if it has one, or the next KQ-1001, KQ-1002… if not, with the same lines, tax and customer. Changing its price before the customer answers updates the estimate. When they accept it on their page, the estimate is marked Accepted (with their name and the day); when they decline, Rejected. A quote that expires, or whose link you turn off, is closed there. When you invoice that job, the invoice in QuickBooks is linked to its estimate, just as if you’d turned the estimate into an invoice in QuickBooks. Only quotes and invoices sent after you connect are copied; click Copy earlier ones too to bring in the rest.

What this job cost shows the price against materials (from the materials list, or the price lines’ costs) and labor. Labor is the hours on visits marked done, times the people on each one, at the labor rate you set. From that it shows your margin.

Estimate and actual, kept apart. The price lines’ costs are the estimate, shown with the margin it would leave. Actual cost is only what really happened: materials used (the materials list and Inventory stock), labor, expenses put on the job in Finance, and overhead. Until something actual is in, the job says No actual costs yet instead of a margin. In Labor rates & overhead set the team’s hourly rate, a different rate for anyone whose hour costs something else, and an overhead percent added on top of what a job cost directly (rent, insurance, trucks, software). Only owners and admins see any of it.

Money on a job. Business money in Finance can go on a job: a receipt for materials, fuel, a sub’s bill, or a cash deposit. On the job, tap + Add an expense (or + Money in), or pick the job under Job when you add money in Finance. One purchase that covers several jobs is split across them (+ Split with another job): give each job its share, or leave a share blank for an even part of what’s left. It stays one entry in Finance. The job counts its share under Expenses in what it cost, a deposit counts as Received so far, and what’s on order for it from suppliers shows as On order. Only business money goes on a job. Owners and admins put it there, and admins see the business money that’s on jobs, but not the rest of the business’s money.

One price list. On any job’s price lines, + From the price list lists everything your team prices: the items on your boards’ Price lists (Bids & Quotes) and your Inventory items, in one list. A line keeps the price it was given, so a quote or invoice never changes by itself. When the price list’s price changes later, the line says Price list now $… · use it; tap it to take the new price. In Finance → Quotes, a waiting quote whose prices have changed since it was sent is marked ⚠ prices changed. An automation can start when a price changes too (A price on the price list changes, with {item}, {was}, {now} and {quotes}, the number of waiting quotes that use it): post it in a feed, or make a task to update those quotes. Only owners and admins see what things cost the business.

Messages to customers

On my way: on a visit, tap On my way and pick about how long you’ll be. Text opens your phone’s Messages app with the message written for you, from your own number. Email sends it under your team’s name, and their reply comes back to you.

Reminders and reviews: owners and admins can turn these on in Settings → Team settings → Customer messages. Customers then get an email reminder from 9am the day before a visit, and get one again if the visit moves. An hour after a job is marked Paid, they get a thank-you with your review link (Google, Yelp…). You can also send either one yourself: Remind the customer on a visit, and Ask for a review on a job once a visit is done. Customers marked do not contact never get any of these.

Texting from your business number (Twilio): an owner or admin can connect your own Twilio account in Apps & Integrations → Text messages: buy a number in Twilio, then paste the Account SID, Auth token and the number. From then on, on my way, reminders and review requests are texted to customers from that number (no phone needed), and every contact shows your texts with them, with a box to send one. Replies come back to the contact, you get a notification, and a text from a number Kloomie doesn’t know becomes a new contact. A customer who replies STOP is marked do not contact; START undoes it. Twilio bills you directly. To text US customers, register your business with the carriers in Twilio (Messaging → Regulatory compliance, called A2P 10DLC); until that’s approved, texts to US numbers may be blocked.

Everything is a field you can rename, hide or add to, and you can add more kinds of work any time from the project’s ••• menu → Add a list.

Technology & software

For a small team building a product. In Boards, click Use a template and pick Technology & Software. You get:

  • Work items on a development board: Backlog, Ready, In progress, Code review, Testing, Ready to release, Done. Each is a story, bug or task, numbered with letters from the project’s name (Atlas Mobile → AM-1), with the developer, priority, estimate in points, labels, its feature and release, acceptance criteria and subtasks, and the branch or pull request. A bug also has severity (how bad, apart from how soon), steps to reproduce, expected and actual, which only show on bugs. The Bugs view lists just the bugs.
  • Features on a roadmap by phase (Foundation, Core product, Collaboration, Launch, Later), with dates, an owner, and the features each one depends on.
  • Releases with a release checklist and release notes.
  • Roadmap timeline: Features open on a timeline from start to target date, grouped by phase. Each bar fills in as its work items get done, and a feature that starts before something it depends on is finished is flagged. Any list with a date can be shown as a timeline from its view’s menu.
  • Sprints: the Sprint view on work items has the backlog on one side and the sprint on the other, grouped by person. Move work in and out with → and ↩. It shows points done, days left, whether you’re on track, and who has more than one person can finish (set per sprint). Finish sprint marks it done and moves what isn’t done to the next sprint (made for you if there isn’t one) or back to the backlog.
  • Docs: 📚 Docs at the top of the project has templates for requirements (PRD), a technical spec, a decision record (“why PostgreSQL, not MongoDB”), a setup guide, a runbook and release notes, kept in the project’s own docs folder. A feature’s doc button writes its requirements, a work item’s writes a spec, and a release’s writes its release notes; the doc stays linked to it.
  • Standup: ☀️ Standup asks each person three things: what they finished, what they’re on today, and whether anything is in their way. Everyone sees the team’s answers for the day, and who hasn’t posted. Something in the way becomes a task for the project lead, who gets a notification. Whoever made the project (or an owner or admin) can turn on a reminder each weekday morning and choose who blockers go to.
  • GitHub: if your team connected GitHub (Apps & Integrations → GitHub), 🐙 GitHub lets you pick a repository, with nothing to paste. Otherwise it gives you a webhook address and secret to paste into your repository’s Settings → Webhooks (pick Pull requests, Pushes and Releases). Then put a work item’s key in a branch name, pull request title or commit message (am-12-login-fix, “Fixes AM-12”): the pull request and commits show on the item, opening a pull request moves it to Code review, and merging moves it to Testing. Cards only move forward, and you can switch moving off. Publishing a release on GitHub marks the release with the same version Released. Each work item has Copy branch name. Kloomie only accepts deliveries signed with your secret.
  • Testing: Test cases have steps, what’s expected, platforms and a result, on a Test run board (Not run, Pass, Fail, Blocked). Marking one Fail makes a bug with its steps, what was expected and what happened, linked to the test (while that bug is open, failing again just notes it on the bug). 🧪 New test run puts the tests back to Not run, for everything or one release.
  • Ready to ship? Each release shows what’s not done yet (its work items, directly or through its features), serious bugs still open, and how its tests went. ✦ Draft release notes has your AI write them from what’s finished, in your company’s voice, with serious open bugs as known issues; save them on the release or make them a doc.
  • ✦ Plan a feature: describe an idea in your own words and Kloomie drafts the feature (phase, priority, what and why) and the work to build it: stories and tasks with points and acceptance criteria, in the order they’d be built, plus the questions to answer first and the risks. Change anything, untick what you don’t want, and Make them puts the feature on the roadmap and the work in the backlog.
  • ✦ Write test cases: on a feature, Kloomie drafts test cases from its work items and their acceptance criteria (with steps and what to expect), skipping ones that already exist. Pick the ones you want; they’re added as Not run, linked to the feature and its release.

Apps & Integrations

Apps & Integrations has its own place in the sidebar, under Workspace. It has three tabs:

  • Apps: every app Kloomie connects to, as tiles grouped by kind (email and calendar, files, money, marketing, AI and more). The ones you’ve connected are at the top. Search, or pick a kind. Tap a tile to see what it does and connect it. Some apps only owners and admins can connect for the team (QuickBooks, Stripe, Twilio, Postiz). Your own calendar, email and AI key are yours to connect.
  • Tools: which of Kloomie’s tools your team uses. Owners and admins switch them on and off. Turning one off hides it for everyone, and nothing in it is deleted.
  • Developers: API keys, webhooks and website lead forms, for your website, Zapier, Make or your own code.

Tiles marked Not built yet are apps Kloomie doesn’t connect to directly yet. Until it does, Zapier or Make can often link them to Kloomie. Tap Want this? on one to tell us you’d use it. The apps people ask for most get built first. Owners and admins see how many on their team asked.

What now: once an app is connected, its page suggests two or three automations that put it to work. For example, with Twilio you can text the customer when a visit is booked; with GitHub you can post merges and releases in #general. Owners and admins tap Turn on, or Edit first to change one before it starts. The card also says where the app’s data will show up.

Needs attention: a tile turns red when a connection stops working, for example when its sign-in expired or the key was changed in the other app. Open it to see what’s wrong. Usually the fix is to connect it again, and nothing already in Kloomie is lost. The person who connected it gets one notice. For a team connection, members just see it as connected; owners and admins see what’s wrong. To do something when it happens, an automation can start from A connected app needs connecting again, with {app} and {error} to fill in.

Automations and team connections: an automation that texts customers through the team’s Twilio, or sends to another app, has to be made by an owner or admin. If its maker stops being one, those steps stop running, and the automation’s log says why.

GitHub

An owner or admin opens Apps & Integrations → GitHub and clicks Connect GitHub. GitHub asks which account or organisation and which repositories; pick them and you’re back in Kloomie. Kloomie only reads: it never changes your code.

  • Home: a Code card shows pushes, merged pull requests and releases since yesterday, each linking to GitHub. Brief me mentions them too.
  • Who sees it: people on software boards (the default), everyone, or only owners and admins. Chief of Staff and Operations can read it for the people who can.
  • Software boards: open a board → GitHub and pick one of the connected repositories. Nothing to paste into GitHub. Work items move as described under Technology & software.
  • Automations: start one when code is pushed, a pull request is opened or merged, or a release is published, with {repo}, {branch}, {text}, {who} and {url} to fill in.
  • One GitHub, more than one team: anyone who can reach the same GitHub installation can connect it to their own Kloomie team, and each team then sees its activity. Only people who already have access to that code on GitHub can do this.
  • Change repositories takes you back to GitHub. If Kloomie is uninstalled or suspended on GitHub, it disconnects in Kloomie too; connect again to bring it back. Disconnect stops Kloomie hearing from GitHub; to remove it from GitHub too, uninstall it in GitHub’s settings.

CompanyCam

Photos your crew takes in CompanyCam show up on the job in Kloomie. An owner or admin opens Apps & Integrations → CompanyCam, makes an access token in CompanyCam (Settings → Integrations → Access tokens; it needs a Pro, Premium or Elite plan), pastes it and taps Connect. Kloomie checks the token and sets up the rest in CompanyCam itself.

  • Projects match jobs by address: a CompanyCam project at the same street address as a job (and the same city, when both have one) is linked to it. When it isn’t clear (two jobs at one address, or none), the project waits on the tile: pick the job (or tap all jobs) and its latest photos come in.
  • Photos land in the job’s Photos, newest first, each one once, so sign-offs, invoices and your AI team (Operations’ photo tools) can use them.
  • Automations can start when a CompanyCam photo lands on a job ({record}, {who} took it, {text} the CompanyCam project) or a CompanyCam project is made or changed.
  • Only owners and admins see the connection’s details; everyone else sees that it’s connected. Disconnect removes Kloomie’s webhook in CompanyCam; photos already on jobs stay.

Microsoft Teams

Kloomie can post to a channel in your Microsoft Teams. There’s no Microsoft app to install. An owner or admin opens Apps & Integrations → Microsoft Teams and follows the steps: in Teams, open the channel’s ⋯ → Workflows, pick “Post to a channel when a webhook request is received”, and copy the web address it shows. Paste it, name the channel, and tap Connect and send a test. Kloomie posts a short hello in the channel to check it works.

  • Automations post with a Post in Microsoft Teams step, with {words} filled in: “New lead: {contact}”, “{record} is {stage}”, “{customer} paid {invoice}”. Ready-made ones are offered right after you connect. Only owners and admins can have an automation post there, and if whoever made one stops being an owner or admin, it stops posting.
  • Your AI team can draft a Teams post; it never goes out until you read it and say yes. Posting from Kloomie by hand (and through your AI team) is for owners and admins unless an admin sets Who can post to everyone; each person can post up to 10 an hour.
  • The address works like a key, so Kloomie keeps it sealed and never shows it again. Everyone else on the team sees only that Teams is connected.
  • If the workflow is deleted or turned off in Teams, the tile says Needs attention and whoever connected it gets a notice: make the workflow again, then Disconnect and paste the new address. Send a test checks it any time. At most 60 posts an hour go out.

Calendar

Go to Apps & Integrations → your calendar and click Connect Google Calendar, Connect Outlook or Connect Apple Calendar. Choose which calendars show in your Day, and which calendar the blocks you make in Kloomie go onto. Only you see your calendar’s events.

Apple Calendar (iCloud): Apple doesn’t let apps sign in to iCloud directly, so you make a password just for Kloomie. You can cancel it at Apple any time.

Email

Open the Email tool and click Connect Gmail or Connect Outlook. Kloomie can then send the email you write from your own address, so replies come back to your inbox and it shows in your Sent folder. Kloomie can’t read your inbox.

Docs and signatures

Open Folders & Docs in Workspace. Start a doc from scratch, from a template like a proposal or call script, or with Draft it with AI. Type / for any kind of block, including live ones like a campaign summary that keeps itself up to date.

Pages inside pages: type /page in a doc, or tap + beside a doc in Docs, to put a page inside it, like a handbook with a chapter for each topic (up to 8 levels deep). The page shows in its doc as a link you tap to open, and the line at the top of each page shows the way back. A page inside a doc is kept where the doc is and seen by the same people: in a private folder it stays private. Move into another doc… in the ••• menu moves a doc, with the pages inside it, into another one (or back out).

Pages beside the doc: on a wide screen an open doc shows its pages as a tree on the left (the top page and every page inside it), plus your starred and recent docs. Fold it away with the button at its top.

A project’s list in a doc: type /project and pick a list from Boards. The doc shows its newest records as a table (or, from the block’s ⋮⋮ menu, a board by stage), kept up to date. Tap one to open it, or type in + New to add one. Only people who can see the project see what’s in it; anyone else sees that a table is there, and a public link never shows it.

Videos, maps and designs: type /embed and paste a YouTube, Vimeo, Loom, Google Maps, Figma or Google Docs/Sheets/Slides/Forms link to show it right in the doc. A public link shows these as links.

Icon and cover: point at the top of a doc for Add icon (an emoji, shown in Docs and on the pages that link to it) and Add cover (a picture across the top).

Search and Ask: press ⌘K (Ctrl+K on Windows), or the search button in Chat, to find docs (the one named for what you typed comes first, with the page it’s inside), records in Boards, files, messages, campaigns, tasks and stacks. Type a question (“what do we charge for shutters?”) and choose ✦ Ask: Kloomie reads the docs and records you can see and answers on your own AI key, with numbered sources to tap. It reads only what you can see, never changes anything, and says so when it can’t find the answer.

A small public site: when a doc has pages inside, Share a public link offers it “with the pages inside, as a small site”: anyone with the link reads the doc and every page inside it, with a contents list (pages added inside later are on it too). Turning the link off takes the whole site down.

Templates: an empty doc offers templates, including ones for trades (Safety talk, New hire onboarding, Price book, Job handover, Site inspection, Job close-out). Save as a team template… in a doc’s ••• menu saves it, with the pages inside it, for the whole team to start from; only a doc the whole team can see can be one.

Coming from Notion: in Notion, go to Settings → Export, choose Markdown & CSV with subpages and Create folders for subpages, and download the .zip. In Kloomie, Docs → Import, pick the .zip and choose who sees it (only you at first, if you like, then move the folder to the team). Every page becomes a doc with its pages inside it, pictures and files come along, and links between pages still work and show under “Linked from”. Databases become boards in Boards, each row a record with its page as the record’s doc (when Boards is on), or tables in a doc. When it’s done, Kloomie lists anything it couldn’t bring in.

Markdown, text and Word files: open one and tap Make it a doc to edit it as a doc (Google Docs come in from Drive as Word files). The file stays as it was.

Starred and trash: star the docs you use most and they sit at the top of Docs (your stars are yours alone). Move to trash takes a doc and the pages inside it out of everything; Undo, or 🗑 Trash in Docs, puts it back where it was. The trash keeps docs for 30 days, then deletes them for good. You see the ones you deleted or made; owners and admins see them all.

From a doc’s ••• menu you can export to Word or PDF, share a public link, see every version, lock it, or send it for signature: put a box where they sign, say who signs, and they get a private link that needs no account. The signed copy comes back to you, with a last page showing who signed, when and from where.

Your team (agents)

Agents in the side toolbar opens Your team: four specialists you hand work to. They work on your own AI key, as you. They see only what you can see, and what they make is real: tasks in your Day, time on your calendar, jobs and deals in Jobs, docs in Docs.

  • 🧭 Chief of Staff: your day and week. Morning briefing, Plan my day, Weekly reset, Inbox to action (paste emails and it turns commitments into tasks), Schedule recovery and an end-of-day summary.
  • 🤝 Client & Sales Manager: leads and customers. Handle a new lead (paste the inquiry), Follow-up radar for deals that went quiet, proposals, meeting prep, a daily sales report, and logging a conversation.
  • 🛠️ Operations Manager: delivering the work. Project kickoff, job preparation, routes, a project health check, work orders and checklists, and closing out a job.
  • 📣 Marketing Manager: bringing in the right customers. A monthly marketing plan, building a campaign (goals, tasks, who to reach, emails and posts), winning back past customers, reviews and referrals, a content week of posts, a neighbor blitz around a job, and a report on what’s working: leads and jobs won by source, and how each campaign did.

Marketing works from your business. It reads your Company HQ profile (voice, ideal customer, what makes you different) and your real results, and never makes up reviews, prices or offers. Pictures for posts and flyers are made on your OpenAI or Google Gemini key (Claude doesn’t make pictures) and kept in Kloomie. Email campaigns go from your Gmail or Outlook with your footer and an unsubscribe link, never to anyone marked do not contact, and only after you say yes.

Calendly and Cal.com bookings come into Kloomie by themselves. Open Apps & Integrations → Calendly (or Cal.com). There are two ways to connect, never mixed: an owner or admin connects the team’s booking page, and anyone can connect their own (only their bookings, and only they can change it; it’s removed if they leave the team). For Calendly, press Connect and sign in to Calendly. For Cal.com, make an API key in Cal.com → Settings → Developer → API keys and paste it. Kloomie sets up the rest itself, so there’s nothing to paste back into Calendly or Cal.com.

  • Who booked goes into Contacts. They’re matched by email, then by phone, so someone already there isn’t added twice. The booking is noted on their contact.
  • The booking goes on the host’s Day and Home. If the person has a job on a board, it’s a visit on the job instead (an “Estimate” booking becomes an Estimate visit). The host hears about it in the bell.
  • Cancels and moves follow: a cancelled booking comes off the Day, and a moved one moves.
  • Automations can start when someone books, cancels or moves (Workspace → Automations, under Bookings). The tile suggests a few right after connecting: a task to get ready, a post to the team, a task to rebook a cancellation.
  • Sidekick and your agents see the day’s bookings: the morning brief mentions them, and the Sales agent’s follow-up radar includes people who just booked. What someone typed when booking is kept as text, and never treated as instructions.
  • If Calendly says your plan doesn’t allow it, the tile says so. Press Check again after changing plans. Disconnect stops new bookings coming in and removes Kloomie’s connection in Calendly or Cal.com. Bookings already in Kloomie stay.

Social and Google posts go out through your own Postiz account (postiz.com, which bills you directly). An owner or admin connects it in Apps & Integrations → Social posts (Postiz): click Connect with Postiz and approve Kloomie on Postiz’s page (or paste the API key from Postiz → Settings → Public API, which is also how you connect a Postiz you host yourself). To stop it, disconnect in Kloomie or remove Kloomie in Postiz → Settings → Approved Apps. Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, X, Threads, Bluesky and Mastodon are supported. Each post waits for your yes: change the words, channels, picture or time, then post it, schedule it or save it as a draft in Postiz. Undo takes a scheduled post back out. Without Postiz, the drafts are still there to copy.

Type what you need in Ask your team and Kloomie works out who does what. For “I just landed a job”, Sales records the customer, Operations sets up the job, work order and visits, and the Chief of Staff fits it into your calendar. Each one hears what the others did. Or open one specialist for their one-tap workflows.

Every assignment shows each step: what it looked at, what it changed and why, with Open to go to it and Undo to take it back. You can reply to carry on the same assignment.

How far each one goes is up to you, per agent, under Settings: Observe (only reports and suggests), Prepare (every change waits for your yes), Execute (the usual: routine changes it makes itself) or Automate (as Execute, plus its Schedules, like a morning briefing at 7 on weekdays). Emails are always drafted for you to read, change and send; nothing goes out without you. Agents never delete anything, and your team’s rules in Company HQ apply to them like anyone else. Things waiting for your yes show on Home and in your notifications. Add standing instructions for each one, like “always offer a free in-home measure”.

Make each one yours. Open an agent, then Settings to rewrite its job (the prompt it works from), or Connections to set what it may use: Off, Read or Read + act for your calendar, tasks, jobs, contacts, email, money and the rest. Your changes are yours alone, and Reset brings the original back. If you pin an agent to a chat, it answers each person with that person’s own job and connections.

Whatever the job says, Kloomie’s rules stay: an agent never deletes, never gets more than you have, and never sends, posts or invoices without your yes. A tool your team switched off, or switched off for members, is off for your agents too. Only owners and admins can post to the company’s social accounts. If you turn a connection off while a draft waits on it, the draft stays until you turn it back on or skip it.

They don’t read your inbox. Paste an email into a workflow instead. An owner can switch Agents off for the team in Settings.

Company HQ

Company HQ (in Settings → Business) is what Kloomie knows about your business: what you do and where, your mission and values, your tone of voice, your goals, your customers, how you work and your rules. Sidekick, Docs and every AI button follow it, so what they write sounds like you and fits how you work.

  • Start from your website: type your site’s address and click Read my site. Kloomie reads your home page and pages like About, Services and FAQ, and suggests entries from what they say, each showing which page it came from. Nothing is added until an owner or admin accepts it, and you can edit a suggestion as you accept it.
  • The interview: under Let’s get to know your business, Kloomie asks one question at a time: your story, what you believe, who you serve, where you’re going, how work gets done and what needs your OK. Each answer can turn into suggestions, shown right under the reply. Stop any time; Start over clears the conversation, not what you’ve accepted.
  • Your documents: upload a price sheet, handbook, proposal template, checklist or agreement (they’re kept in a Company HQ folder), or pick files already in Kloomie. Kloomie suggests your prices, policies, steps and rules, each saying which file it came from.
  • Values that do something: a value can say what it looks like in practice and what to avoid (“Never pushy: give people time to decide; no countdown deals”), so the AI knows how to act on it.
  • Rules: say what Kloomie may do without asking, what needs an owner’s OK, and what it must never do.
  • Each job gets what it needs: Kloomie doesn’t hand the AI your whole profile every time. An email to a customer gets your voice, your customer values and what never to promise; a plan gets your goals and rules; an estimate gets your prices and policies; team notes get your team values. Tag a value with where it matters (sales, customers, jobs…) to keep it where it belongs. When something a job needs isn’t in your profile yet, like your prices for an estimate, the AI says so instead of guessing.
  • Rules Kloomie enforces: in Rules & boundaries, an owner chooses what needs an OK and what isn’t allowed: giving a discount over a percent, sending an invoice over an amount, deleting a job or record. These aren’t just instructions for the AI. When someone who can’t approve tries one, nothing changes yet: the owners get a notification and a Needs you item, and Approve makes the change exactly as it was asked (or says why it couldn’t, if the thing is gone). Decline can include a note, and the person who asked hears either way. Owners can always act; choose whether admins can approve too.
  • Learning from your edits: when someone changes an AI email, document or call script before using it, Kloomie keeps the draft and what was used. Every three, it looks for what keeps changing (shorter, no exclamation marks, the same sign-off) and suggests it as a writing preference, marked “learned from your edits”. Small typo fixes teach nothing, and nothing changes until an owner or admin accepts it.
  • Are these still your priorities? Every three months, owners and admins get a Needs you item to check the goals. Still right resets the clock; changing a goal does too.
  • The Company Playbook: Make the Playbook turns everything approved into one document your team can read: who you are, why you exist, what you never compromise, where you’re going, who you serve, how you sound, how you work and how you decide, plus what’s still missing. It’s kept in the Company HQ folder; making it again updates the same doc, and its history keeps the earlier versions.
  • The meter shows the essentials still missing. Owners and admins change Company HQ; everyone on the team can read it.

Finance

Open Finance from Workspace → Tools. On a wide screen the Overview puts the month (money in and out, budgets, your pipeline) beside the bills coming up. Log money in and out (tag it to a campaign to count it as that campaign’s cost), or import the CSV statement your bank lets you download: Kloomie sorts each line into a category and remembers how you sorted the same place before. Set monthly budgets, and add bills so they show on your Day and on Home when they’re due. Business money is shared with the team’s owners and admins; personal money is only yours. Members don’t see the business’s money.

Receipts. When you add or open money in or out, tap + Add a receipt to keep a photo (from your phone’s camera or your files) or a PDF with it, up to 10 per entry and 8 MB each. A receipt is seen by exactly who sees its entry: the business’s by owners and admins, your personal money’s only by you. Receipts aren’t put in the team’s files. In & out shows 📎 on entries that have one, and deleting an entry deletes its receipts.

Invoices and Quotes (owners and admins) are tabs in Finance too. Invoices shows what customers owe you, what’s overdue and what was paid this month, then every invoice (Owed, Overdue, Drafts, Paid, All) and the payments that came in; tap one to open it. Quotes shows every quote the team has sent, from a job or from Bids & Quotes: what’s waiting on an answer, what was accepted, and your win rate; tap one to open it. If you’re an owner or admin and keep no personal money here, Finance starts on Business.

Reports (owners and admins) shows the business’s money for a period: this month, last month, this or last quarter, this or last year. You see money in, money out, and profit (money in, less what’s deductible), money in by category, and your spending by category with the tax category it goes under. Kloomie guesses a tax category from the name (Fuel goes under Car & truck); pick another and it’s kept for that category. Set the share of profit you want to set aside for taxes (ask your accountant what fits you; it’s a reminder, not a tax calculation). Miles driven come from Map → Mileage. Export for your accountant downloads the period’s business money as a spreadsheet (CSV), with each line’s tax category.

  • Ask it for anything: “plan my week”, “follow up with customers who went quiet”, “organize this job”. Your team (the Chief of Staff, Sales, Operations and Marketing specialists) does the work in your tasks, calendar, jobs, contacts, campaigns and docs, and you watch each step land in the panel with Open and Undo.
  • It knows what you’re looking at. The panel says “Looking at …” (a job, a campaign, a doc, your Day) so “this” means something. Tap Leave it out to ask without it.
  • How can Kloomie help you? Tell Sidekick what you want: “Help me grow from $20k to $40k a month by June”, “Help me get more storefront customers”, or pick a goal: Grow / scale, More leads & marketing, Run my operations, Get my time back, Money or My team (each fills in a sentence for you to finish). For each goal Sidekick knows what it needs to find out (for growth: what you bring in now, your target, your average job, how many jobs your team can take), asks you for anything it can’t find, then lays out a plan: campaigns with numbers to hit, a weekly schedule, budgets, tasks, invoices, Company HQ entries and a one-page plan doc with the numbers to watch. Nothing changes until you approve: say yes to each piece (or Approve all), skip what you don’t want, and undo anything later.
  • Plans: Plan a campaign, Plan my schedule and Plan my budget talk it through with you and lay out cards to accept, edit or skip before anything is built.
  • Running the work: your team can also draft an invoice from a job, send it, send a customer a job’s sign-off to sign online, and add what it learns to Company HQ. Drafting an invoice is routine; sending an invoice or a sign-off and changing Company HQ always wait for your yes.

It waits in Your team for your yes; once you say yes it’s made and switched on as drafted, and undo deletes it. Sending to another app is set up by a person in the builder, never by an agent.

Sidekick runs on your own AI key from Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI or Google (Gemini), so you choose the model and pay that company directly for what you use. Add a key in Apps & Integrations → AI. Your key is yours: nobody else on the team sees it.

Inventory

What the business keeps and where it is lives in Workspace → Tools → Inventory. An item has a name, SKU, category, unit, cost and price, a reorder point, a supplier and notes. Places are where stock sits: a warehouse, a truck (with its driver), a job site or anywhere else; Main storage is made with your first item. Each item shows how many are at each place.

On a computer, New item and New place open as full pages with a live preview beside the form: how many are on hand, when it will show as low, the supplier and, for owners and admins, cost, value and margin. A place’s kind (warehouse, truck, job site and so on) is a card to tap. On a phone they’re one column.

  • Moving stock: Receive what arrives, Move it between places, Use it on a job or task (with a note), Bring back what wasn’t used, Adjust (say why) or Count (sets the real number and logs the difference). A move never takes a place below zero: it tells you there are only so many there.
  • Low stock: an item at or under its reorder point is marked red, with a Low stock filter and a summary at the top.
  • Activity lists every move, with who did it and when.
  • Equipment, one by one: a new item can be tracked One by one instead of by count, for gear with serials. Each unit has a name, serial, its own QR label, a status (Available, Out, In repair, Lost, Retired), where it is, and who has it and for which job while it’s out. On a unit: Check out (to someone, for a job if you like), Bring back, Move, 📍 It’s here (your phone’s location), and Report a problem / Fixed. Owners and admins also mark it Lost or Found, retire it, edit it and print its label.
  • Lock combos are kept sealed: anyone on the team can tap Show, and every look is logged (who and when, never the combo).
  • Labels: print them with 🏷 from the item. Scanning one with a phone’s camera opens the unit in Inventory.
  • Coming from an Equipment & assets project? Owners and admins see Move into Inventory at the top: one click brings each record in as a unit (one item per kind), keeping its status, where it is, the job, who has it, the combo and its label, so the stickers already on your gear keep working. The project is then archived, so the gear lives in one place: it’s under Archived in Boards, and opened, it says the gear is kept in Inventory now, with Open Inventory and Restore (to bring the project back).
  • On a job: its 🧰 Materials & equipment box shows the stock used on it (less what came back; costs only for owners and admins) and the equipment out at it, with Use stock and Check out equipment right there. What a job cost includes the stock used on it. On a task, its ⋯ menu has Use stock for it… (counted on the task, and on its job if it has one).
  • Automations: they can start when an item runs low (once, as it reaches its reorder point), when stock is used on a job, or when equipment is checked out, brought back or reported with a problem, with the words {item}, {qty}, {unit}, {place} and {supplier}. A step can Use or move stock: use it on the job, move it between places, or (owners and admins) receive it. Ready-made: “Running low? Reorder it” and “Equipment problem? Get it fixed”.
  • Your AI team can look up what’s on hand where, and Operations can record stock used on a job.
  • Reordering: the Reorder tab lists everything at or under its reorder point, grouped by supplier, with how many to order (the item’s reorder amount, or enough to get comfortably over the point) and, for owners and admins, an estimate. For each supplier: Copy the order, Email it (your email app opens with the order written out) or Make it a task. Received on a line logs it coming in.
  • Requests: anyone can tap Request items. Owners and admins choose who hears about requests (and a feed for them, like a private #inventory), then approve, decline, hand it out or order it. Whoever asked hears about each step.
  • Supplier orders: owners and admins can tap Place the order… on a supplier’s group. The amounts are filled in (change any of them), and you can add a note or PO number. Orders are numbered and wait under On order. An item that’s already on order says “N on order” in the low list and isn’t suggested again. Receive… puts what came into a place you pick. Change a number if only part of it arrived, and the rest stays on order. The order is done once everything’s in, or you can Cancel the order. Members see orders but not their costs.
  • QR labels: every item and every place can have a printed label. Print one from the item, from the place, or many at once from Inventory’s ⋯ menu → Print QR labels…, on a Letter sheet (sticker paper, three across; print at 100%) or for a 2×1" label printer. Scanning a label with a phone’s camera opens that item or place in Kloomie.
  • Places: click a place on the Places tab to see what’s there, with Use, Move, Receive here, Put away here and Count this place. Show in the list filters the main list to that place, as before.
  • Counts and spreadsheets (Inventory’s ⋯ menu): Count a place… (owners and admins type what’s really there; only the differences are saved, as counts in each item’s history), Import a spreadsheet… (a CSV with headings like Item, SKU or Part number, Supplier or Vendor, Qty or On hand, Location, Reorder point; it matches items by SKU, then name, and makes any new places) and Export a spreadsheet (what’s on hand at each place; costs only for owners and admins).
  • Everyone on the team sees what’s where and can move, use or bring back stock. Owners and admins add items and places, receive, adjust and count, and see costs, prices and what the stock is worth. Switch Inventory off for the team in Apps & Integrations → Tools.

Templates

Wording your team sends again and again lives in Workspace → Tools → Templates: emails (with a subject), text messages, replies and social posts, plus your paperwork (doc templates and sign-off forms). Make one, import one from a file (.txt, .docx or a web page), or have Sidekick write some for you. Write {customer}, {record}, {date} and other words for what changes each time; tap a word to add it where your cursor is. Anyone on the team can make a template; only whoever made it, or an owner or admin, can change or delete it.

  • In automations: an email, text or post step has Use a template…: pick one and its wording is copied into the step, where the words get filled in each time it runs.
  • Your AI team uses them: before writing an email, text, reply, post or document, your agents look for a template that fits and start from it, filling in the details. They can also save new ones (it shows in Your team, and undo removes it), and a goal plan can propose the templates you’ll need.
  • Paperwork: tap a doc template to make a new doc from it. Save any doc as a template from its ⋯ menu; sign-off forms are added on a job’s sign-off field.

Automations

Owners and admins can set up “when this happens, do that” in Workspace → Automations. For example: when a step is done, make a follow-up task; when a contact becomes a customer, tell the owners; every Monday at 9, post in #general.

One step of a campaign: automations about tasks and steps can be narrowed to a Step by its name, as well as to a campaign. A task opens starts one when what a task waited for is done, so it can be worked. Notify → Whoever the task is for reaches its person, or everyone in its role. {done} is yes or no, so “wait 2 days, then only if {done} is no” reminds people only when it’s still open.

Ready-made automations: start from one made for your kind of business. For you shows the ones for your team’s kind first; chips show Trades & field service, Services & appointments, Sales & leads, Office & money, Shops, Software & tech or All, and each card lists the steps it uses. A few: “Quote accepted? Book the install”, “Done? Bill it and ask for a review”, “Visit booked? Text the customer”, “New lead? Sales drafts a reply”, “Paid? Thank them”, “Friday recap from your team” and “New job? Tell another app”.

Start from a recipe or build your own. Each automation keeps a log of what it did. If one fails, a dot shows on the Automations tab.

When something fails: if another app it sends to is down or slow to answer, the step is tried again 1, 5 and 30 minutes later, from that step, so the steps before it aren’t done twice (each try carries the same Idempotency-Key, so the other app can tell). If it still fails, or fails for a reason trying again won’t fix (the app said no, a job was held back, its list is gone), it stops, says why in its log, and whoever made it gets a notification.

What can set one off: something from any tool. Tasks and contacts; calls, check-ins and finished campaigns; a job or record added, moved to a stage (however it got there: in the app, through the API or a customer answering a quote) or deleted; a quote accepted or declined; a visit scheduled, done or cancelled; an invoice sent or paid and a payment in (only over an amount, if you like); the business’s money in or out; files and signatures; a text from a customer; a new doc; a pin dropped on the Map for a campaign; or a schedule. Narrow it to one project and stage, one campaign, or an amount.

Dates on records: A date on a job or record comes up goes off a number of days before, on or after a date in a list: 30 days before a license expires, 14 days before a plan renews, the day after a job for a review. Before a date, it goes off on any day up to the date itself, so a license added with 10 days left still warns. It goes off once for each date, and again if the date changes.

Changing records: a step can Change the job or record that set it off (assign whoever moved it, set a date like today+3, fill in a field with {words}), or Add a job or record to any list you can see. A record it adds is for the same customer and links back when it can. It sets plain fields only, never price lines (they need their own OKs). Each change goes on the record’s timeline under the automation’s name, and what an automation adds never sets off another automation.

The builder: opening an automation shows it across the page beside the sidebar: what sets it off at the top, and its steps flowing down under it. Only if splits into Then and Otherwise side by side (one under the other on a phone). Tap + between steps to add one there, or drag a step by ⋮⋮ anywhere, into a branch too. A step’s ⋯ menu has Duplicate and Move up/down, and ▾ folds it (or Fold all). A step that still needs something is marked red. Tap a word chip to put {word} where your cursor is. Leaving with changes not saved keeps you there and asks you to save or close. ▶ Test it walks the automation through the last real event (or a made-up one), lights the way it would go and says what each step would do, without doing anything; Show a past run lights the way a real run went.

Running the job: a step can also Book a visit on the job, Bill the job (an invoice draft, or ready to send), Text the customer, Email the customer, Add money to Finance (the business’s, so owners only) or Write a doc from a template. Anything that reaches a customer (a text, an email, sending an invoice) always waits for an OK: it shows on Home and in the bell as “Automation … needs your OK”, and whoever made the automation can OK it too. Nobody marked do not contact, or who unsubscribed, gets anything. Texting needs texting connected (Apps & Integrations → Twilio). Billing and messaging customers is for owners and admins.

Private and outside: a step can Put a sealed note in a vault (for whoever made the automation, whoever set it off, or someone you name): it’s sealed before it’s saved, so nobody, Kloomie included, can read it. They get a notification, and it becomes an item in their vault the next time they unlock it. A vault takes these once its owner has opened it since this came out, and starting a vault over throws away notes still waiting. A step can also Send to another app: what happened, sent as JSON to a public https address, with a key if that app needs one (add it right in the step with + Add a key…; it’s sealed and only its last four characters are ever shown again). Sending to another app is for owners and admins; if its key is deleted, the automation stops and says why.

Ask your team (AI): a step can hand something to one of your agents, or your whole team, on your own AI key. They’re told what happened and that an automation asked, and they work at the level you set for each agent, so anything important still waits for your yes in Your team, where the assignment shows with the automation’s name. Tick Wait for the answer and the steps after it wait until the agent is done (up to 30 minutes), then can use {answer}, in Only if too; if it takes longer, they go on without it and say so. Without an AI key the step fails and says why. What an agent does never sets off another automation.

Deciding and waiting: a step can be Only if… (for example only if {amount} is over 1000, or {stage} is Lead), with its own steps and an Otherwise; and Wait… from a minute to 90 days, then carry on (“wait 2 days, then check in”). After a wait it acts on things as they are by then: a contact renamed meanwhile is named as they are now. Runs that are waiting show on the automation, and you can stop one. Changing an automation’s steps stops the runs waiting in the old ones.

What stays private: something in a private campaign, project or folder only sets off an automation narrowed to that campaign or project, made by someone who can see it. The business’s money only sets off owners’ automations, and nobody’s own money ever does.

News

Your own news: current events and articles from the topics you pick, from publishers’ public feeds. Turn it on in Settings → Preferences → News on Home; Open News on its Home card (or in Settings) shows everything. It’s yours alone: nobody on your team sees what you read, like or save.

  • Pick what you follow in Topics & sources: top stories, business, small business, your trade (construction, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, solar, landscaping, pest control, cleaning, pools, auto repair, trucking, salons, restaurants…), tech, sports and more. Add words to follow (they come first) or hide, and your own sites: your local paper or a trade blog. Paste the site’s address and Kloomie finds its feed.
  • 👍 and 👎 teach it. A like brings more stories with those words, from that source and topic; “Not for me” takes the story out and brings fewer like it (with Undo). In an article’s ⋯ menu you can ask for more or fewer from a source, or hide it. What it learned shows what it took from you, and Start over forgets it.
  • ☆ Save keeps an article under ★ Saved. Articles open on the publisher’s site in a new tab, and pictures come through Kloomie, so publishers don’t see who’s reading.
  • ✦ Brief me sums up your top stories in a few lines. Without an AI key of your own, Kloomie writes it for free, 3 a day each (the brief says how many are left; a team shares a daily limit too). With your own key (Apps & Integrations → AI) there’s no daily limit, just a pause if you ask for a lot in one hour.
  • Home shows News for you, your top few, while News on Home is on. Owners and admins can switch News off for the whole team in Apps & Integrations → Tools.

Two-step sign-in

Turn it on in Settings → Profile & sign-in → Two-step sign-in. After your password, Kloomie then asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password, Authy and others), so someone who learns your password still can’t get in. Click Turn on, scan the QR code with the app (or type in the key it shows), and enter the code the app gives you. You’ll get 10 backup codes: keep them somewhere safe, like your password manager. If you lose your phone, sign in with a backup code instead; each works once. New backup codes replaces them, and Turn off needs your password and a code. It covers every team you sign in to with that email. Logins without an email add one first.
